    Additional School Candidates for Runchen (Gap Analysis 2026)

    Answering "what other schools would fit Runchen?" by first identifying what the current list structurally lacks, then proposing candidates that fill that gap. Written 2026-07-25. See runchen-college-preferences-overall and runchen-college-ranking-rubric-and-strategy-2026.

    The gap in the current list¶

    His two most-stated criteria are small, campus-centered, "won't feel lost" (counselor's read: under ~10,000 undergrad, per runchen-college-counseling-working-school-list-student-profile) and medical engineering that "needs to involve electricity". Sorting the list by size exposes the problem:

    Size band Schools on the list
    Under 3,000 Harvey Mudd only (~900)
    3,000–10,000 Tufts (~7,600), UPenn (~9,800), Case Western, WPI, RPI, Santa Clara, BU/RIT (over, ~15–19k)
    Over 20,000 Berkeley, UCLA, UCSD, UC Davis, UW Seattle, Georgia Tech, Michigan, UIUC, Cal Poly SLO

    The finding: his single strongest stated preference is served by exactly one school, Harvey Mudd — a 12%-admit reach he has himself twice flagged as possibly "too hard." Nearly half the list is over 20k. If Mudd doesn't land, there is no small-school outcome. That is the real hole, not a missing big public like Wisconsin.

    A second, smaller gap: the counselor recommended private over public for him (less capacity-constraint pressure, more collaborative culture, smaller cohorts), yet the list has drifted heavily public.

    Recommendation 1 — Olin College of Engineering (the strongest single addition)¶

    378 undergraduates. All engineering. ~19–22% admit rate. Needham, MA (Boston metro).

    Why this is the best fit on the board for him: - Project-based from day one, which matches how he actually works. Students "begin project-based engineering in their first semester"; year-one machine shop training and the required Design Nature course (build mechanical toys modeled on biological mechanisms — e.g. a click beetle's jump). Many courses use a "do-learn" approach, applying ideas before theory is formally presented. For a 7-year VEX designer-builder-programmer (runchen-robotics-competition-journey) this is the closest curricular match to his demonstrated strength of any school considered. - Both of his interests are directly served. Olin offers an ABET-accredited Electrical and Computer Engineering degree, plus a Bioengineering concentration (E:Bio) — "the intersection of biology, medicine, engineering and computing." There is also an Engineering Robotics (E:ROBO) concentration. Note: no standalone CS degree. - 378 students and 9:1 student-faculty solves his stated social anxiety — the lecture-hall anonymity fear (runchen-college-counseling-working-school-list-student-profile) is structurally impossible here. - "Collaborative not competitive" is the institutional design, which directly addresses his CMU/Mudd "too hard" worry: he wants intensity without a frenetic, zero-sum feel. - ~19–22% admit is far more attainable than Harvey Mudd's 12% — so it de-risks the small-school outcome rather than duplicating a reach. - Every admitted student receives the Olin Tuition Scholarship. Important caveat: this is now $10,000/year, not free or half tuition — trustees reduced it for the cohort entering September 2026. Older reputation ("Olin is free") is out of date and should not drive the decision. - Cross-registration at Babson, Wellesley, and Brandeis partly offsets the narrowness of a 378-person all-engineering school.

    Real drawbacks to weigh: no CS degree; tiny alumni network; Candidates' Weekend attendance is mandatory and "provides the basis for final admission decisions" (a travel cost and a live-interview burden for a self-described shy student — this cuts both ways); demonstrated interest matters considerably; and cold New England winters, which is a genuine issue for him. Deadline Dec 15 via Common App.

    Recommendation 2 — Rice University (a better-fit Hi Reach than several already on the list)¶

    ~8.0% admit (Class of 2029: 2,948 of 36,791), SAT middle 50% 1510–1560, class size 1,263. Houston, TX.

    • His 1550 sits squarely in Rice's band — mid-range, not a stretch.
    • Residential college system + "an intimate environment that favors collaboration over competition" (Rice Engineering's own framing) is the rare combination of elite-engineering and small-feel that he says he wants. This is what he liked about Cornell, delivered at a smaller scale.
    • Bioengineering is top-10 nationally with "real-world engineering design experiences that begin as early as the freshman year and extend throughout the senior year" — design-forward, like Olin and Wisconsin BME.
    • Houston = the Texas Medical Center, the world's largest medical complex (Baylor College of Medicine, Texas Children's, MD Anderson). For medical-device interest this is the best clinical adjacency of any school on his list. Caveat: Rice's own pages tie TMC collaboration explicitly to the PhD program; undergraduate access is plausible but I could not verify it — see open questions.
    • Warm weather — with the CA schools, one of very few on his list that isn't cold. Directly addresses a recurring complaint.
    • Test-recommended (not required), superscored — favorable for a 1550 superscore. ED I Nov 1 (binding), ED II Jan 4, RD Jan 4.

    Strategic argument: at ~8% Rice is as selective as UCLA/Berkeley/Georgia Tech-OOS, which he ranks 3, but it fits his stated criteria far better than any of them. If the Hi Reach tier needs trimming, Rice is a better use of an application than a Rank-3 mega-public.

    Also worth a look (not researched in depth)¶

    • Rose-Hulman (Terre Haute, IN) — ~2,000 undergrad, all-STEM, undergraduate-teaching focused, has BME and EE. Would be a Likely-tier small school, filling the same gap as Olin at lower selectivity. Strong candidate to replace a drop candidate like RIT or Santa Clara. Could not verify size/admit rate — their site returned 404s.
    • Johns Hopkins — the #1 undergraduate BME program with deep clinical integration; ~6,000 undergrad. Fits the medical-device interest better than almost anywhere. But BME is famously intense and capacity-limited, which collides with his "too hard" sensitivity. Worth researching properly.
    • Northeastern — co-op model would suit a builder, but ~21k undergrad fails the size test.

    What I would actually do¶

    1. Add Olin. It is the highest-value addition: it fixes the structural gap (small-school outcome depends on one 12% reach), matches his hands-on strength better than anything else, serves both ECE and bio interests, and is materially more attainable than Mudd.
    2. Add Rice, and consider it a replacement for a Rank-3 mega-public rather than an addition — better fit at comparable selectivity, plus warm weather.
    3. Research Rose-Hulman as a small-school Likely, potentially swapping out a Rank-4 drop candidate.
    4. Do not add more big publics. Michigan, UIUC, UW, and four UCs already over-serve a category he consistently ranks 3–4 (this is also why Wisconsin was declined).

    Open questions¶

    • Olin's current admit rate and tuition — my figures are Fall 2022/2023 (19%/21.7%) from a secondary source, and Olin publishes no class-profile page I could find. Verify the 2026-27 tuition and confirm the $10,000 scholarship figure directly, since the economics changed recently and materially.
    • Does Rice offer undergraduates real Texas Medical Center access (research, design clients, internships)? Rice's own pages tie TMC to the PhD program. This is the crux of Rice's medical-device appeal and is currently unverified.
    • Rice's Oshman Engineering Design Kitchen — widely known as a hub for undergraduate medical-device design projects with clinical clients, but the OEDK pages returned no content when fetched. Worth confirming, as it would strengthen the Rice case considerably.
    • Rose-Hulman — needs full verification of enrollment, admit rate, cost, BME/EE offerings, and deadlines.
    • Would Runchen accept a 378-person school? Olin is dramatically smaller than anything he has considered; "small" at 378 is a different experience from Tufts at 7,600. Worth asking him directly before adding it.
    • Does mandatory Candidates' Weekend help or hurt him? For a student worried he's "too shy to meet people," a weekend-long collaborative evaluation is either an excellent showcase of his building ability or a significant disadvantage. Judgment call for him and the counselor.
