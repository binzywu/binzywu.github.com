    What Each College on Runchen's List Values Most (Fall 2027 Cycle)

    Companion to runchen-college-list-draft-2026 and runchen-college-essay-requirements-master-list-fall-2027-cycle: for every school on the list, what the institution says it weighs most, and separately what the engineering school specifically requires or values. Researched 2026-07-25 for Runchen's Fall-2027 cycle.

    How to read this¶

    Two kinds of evidence, and they are not equally reliable:

    1. Common Data Set section C7 — the authoritative one. Every college self-reports each factor as Very Important / Important / Considered / Not Considered. It is a ranked, comparable, audited-ish disclosure, not marketing. Section C8A gives the testing policy; C5 gives required high-school units. Where C7 is quoted below it is verbatim from the school's own CDS.
    2. "What we look for" prose — useful for tone and for engineering-specific requirements, but every school says it wants curious, rigorous, community-minded students. Treat prose as tiebreak color, C7 as the actual weighting.

    C7 covers general admission — it explicitly excludes "programs with specific criteria." That is exactly why the engineering-school sections below matter: at CMU, Cornell, Cal Poly, and Georgia Tech the engineering rules override the C7 table.

    One thing C7 cannot tell you: it says character/personal qualities is "Very Important" at six of his hardest schools, but not which qualities. That question is answered separately in applicant-characteristics-each-college-expects-fall-2027-cycle — where only MIT, Cornell, Olin, and CMU turn out to publish an explicit named trait list.

    The five things that matter most in that table¶

    1. MIT's only "Very Important" factor in the entire table is character/personal qualities. Rigor, GPA, test scores, essays, and recommendations are all merely "Important." And MIT is the one school on the list where C8A says SAT/ACT is "Required to be considered for admission" (scores due Feb 15). So MIT gates on tests, then decides on character — his 1550 gets him read, and the essays/recs decide it.
    2. Rice is the most test-weighting school on the list — all six academic factors are "Very Important," test scores included. His 1550 (mid-band for Rice) is worth more here than anywhere else.
    3. Residency is a top-tier factor at three schools, and it cuts both ways. "Very Important" at UW (he's a WA resident — a real advantage), and "Very Important" at Georgia Tech and RIT, where he isn't. GT states it plainly outside the CDS too: "One of the biggest impacts on likelihood of admission (admit rate) for a student is residency" — they target a Georgia-resident majority. That's a structural headwind at the school he currently ranks 1.
    4. Recommendations split sharply. "Very Important" at Cornell, Rice, Tufts, and Harvey Mudd; only "Considered" at Georgia Tech and Berkeley; "Not Considered" at UW — and UCLA "does not require or accept letters of recommendation" at all. Teacher-rec effort should be aimed at the first group.
    5. Demonstrated interest is mostly a myth on this list. "Not Considered" at MIT, Cornell, CMU, Harvey Mudd, and UW; USC states flatly that it "does not conduct admission interviews, nor do we track any form of demonstrated interest." It is "Considered" only at Tufts, Rice, Case Western, RIT, Santa Clara, and RPI — and it "matters considerably" at Olin, which also requires Candidates' Weekend.

    What engineering schools value — the cross-cutting pattern¶

    Six themes recur across every engineering college researched. These are the things a general admissions page won't tell you.

    1. Calculus and physics, explicitly. The engineering colleges that publish coursework expectations almost all name calculus:

    School Engineering-specific coursework expectation
    Boston University (ENG) Applicants "are required to have a year of calculus"
    Santa Clara (eng/sci/business) "readiness at the level of calculus is expected"; 3 yrs math required, 4 recommended
    Olin advises "a solid base in calculus and physics"
    RIT (Kate Gleason) 4 yrs math through pre-calculus, calculus preferred; chemistry AND physics both required
    CMU (CoE) math "preferably calculus"; "Advanced mathematics courses are strongly encouraged, especially a course in calculus"
    Georgia Tech 4 math units required — but calculus is never mentioned anywhere

    This is the single most important finding for Runchen. Bellevue HS offers no AP Calculus BC, so his calculus is Advanced Calculus (MAH802) senior year — see bellevue-school-district-math-pathway-and-advanced-calculus. He satisfies "a year of calculus" and "readiness at the level of calculus," but the course carries a non-AP name. Any school that reads for AP Calc AB/BC by title needs the counselor's course-availability statement doing work in the file.

    2. Science distribution is stricter than the university's. CMU's College of Engineering wants 1 chemistry, 1 physics and 1 biology; its School of Computer Science wants 1 physics plus 2 years of chem/bio/CS. RIT requires chemistry and physics, and Biomedical Engineering additionally requires biology — directly relevant given his medical-device interest.

    3. Several schools admit by major, so the major choice is an admissions decision. Cal Poly SLO: "We evaluate and admit students by their major... applicants compete against other applicants within the same major" (and only 9th–11th grade weighted GPA counts — senior year is excluded, extra weight capped at 8 semesters). Tufts: "candidates are only considered for admission to the School to which they apply." Georgia Tech weighs "the major you put on your application and evidence of your interest in this major." Santa Clara: "academic readiness for the program of interest will be gauged based on your expressed interest." USC: "Careful attention is paid to preparation for the intended major." CMU's own C7 carries the note "Factors vary by program – please see CMU admissions website for program-specific requirements."

    4. The math/science teacher recommendation. Cornell Engineering: "One recommendation must be from a math, science, or computer science teacher." RPI: one rec, "preferably a math or science teacher who knows you well." Rice: teacher letters "preferably with one connected to the student's intended field." Case Western wants a counselor rec plus two teacher recs. Plan the asks accordingly.

    5. Engineering testing policy can differ from the university's. The sharpest case: Cornell Engineering requires scores — "All first-year applicants are required to submit standardized test scores when applying to enroll for fall 2026 and beyond" — even though Cornell's university-wide C7 rates test scores only "Considered." At CMU, the College of Engineering is "test flexible" (scores required, applicant chooses the test) while the School of Computer Science outright requires SAT or ACT.

    6. The stated personality differs by school, and it's a real signal for essay voice. Olin wants "bold, entrepreneurial risk-takers"; RPI wants "creative, collaborative risk-takers... Come build with us"; Cornell Engineering describes itself as "a highly collaborative and dynamic intellectual community known for maintaining all-around excellence"; Tufts lists "Intellectually Playful. Kind. Collaborative. Civically Engaged. Globally Minded."; Georgia Tech wants "students who demonstrate intellectual curiosity and aren't afraid of a challenge."

    Per-school detail¶

    Cornell — Engineering (Hi Reach, Rank 1)¶

    • C7 (2024-25): VI = rigor, essay, recommendations, extracurriculars, talent/ability, character. Only "Important" = class rank, GPA. Test scores "Considered." Interest = Not Considered.
    • Engineering: requires test scores for fall 2026 and beyond. Two teacher recs, one from a math, science, or CS teacher. Two 200-word essays (why engineering / why this college) + four 100-word short answers. ED Nov 1 / RD Jan 2.
    • Read: the most balanced VI profile on the list — six factors tied at the top. Nothing carries him alone; the recs and the engineering essays are weighted as heavily as the transcript. See cornell-university-college-research.

    MIT (Hi Reach, Rank 2)¶

    • C7: character/personal qualities is the sole "Very Important" factor. All academics "Important"; class rank "Considered"; interest Not Considered.
    • C8A: SAT or ACT "Required to be considered for admission" — scores by Feb 15.
    • Read: the transcript and 1550 are a threshold, not the argument. MIT's decision is made on who he is — which puts unusual weight on the short-answer essays and the VEX story in runchen-robotics-competition-journey. See mit-college-research.

    Carnegie Mellon (Hi Reach, Rank 3)¶

    • C7: VI = rigor, class rank, GPA, extracurriculars, character, volunteer work. Essay and recommendations only "Important." Test scores "Considered." Interview and interest Not Considered. Plus the note that factors vary by program.
    • Engineering: CoE units = 4 English, 4 math, 1 chemistry, 1 physics, 1 biology, 2 foreign language, 3 electives. SCS = 4 English, 4 math, 1 physics, 2 yrs chem/bio/CS, 2 foreign language, 3 electives. Math "at least algebra, geometry, trigonometry, analytic geometry, elementary functions (pre-calculus) and preferably calculus." AP credit for 4s and 5s; IB for 6s/7s HL. CoE is test-flexible; SCS requires SAT or ACT.
    • Read: the only school that rates volunteer work "Very Important" — a genuine gap in his profile worth checking against runchen-soccer-and-school-clubs. Also note CMU rates the essay lower than most peers. See carnegie-mellon-university-college-research.

    UC Berkeley / UCLA / UC San Diego / UC Davis (Hi Reach & Target)¶

    • Shared framework: the UC system's 13 comprehensive-review factors are explicitly unranked ("Some factors we may consider are:"), and campuses "often apply these factors differently." Berkeley's five reader criteria "carry no pre-assigned weights."
    • Berkeley C7 (verified): VI = rigor, GPA, application essay. Important = extracurriculars, character, volunteer work, work experience. Considered = recommendations, talent, first generation, state residency. Not Considered = class rank, interview, alumni relation, level of interest. C8A: "Not considered for admission, even if submitted."
    • Berkeley: requires a 3.4 A-G GPA for non-residents vs 3.0 for residents.
    • UCLA: "does not require or accept letters of recommendation"; no interviews; four PIQs at 350 words. "IGETC is not recommended for applicants to the School of Engineering and Applied Sciences."
    • UCSD: "does not consider SAT or ACT test scores as a factor in admissions or scholarship decisions."
    • Read: for all four, the four PIQs plus the A-G transcript are the entire application. No recs, no interview, no scores, no demonstrated interest. This is the highest essay-leverage cluster on his list. See uc-berkeley-college-research, ucla-college-research, uc-san-diego-college-research, uc-davis-college-research.

    Georgia Tech (Hi Reach, Rank 1)¶

    • C7: VI = rigor, GPA, character, state residency. Important = essay, extracurriculars. Considered = test scores, recommendations, talent, geographical residence, volunteer, work. Not Considered = class rank, interview, first generation, alumni, level of interest.
    • C4/C5: requires a college-prep program; 17 total units — English 4, math 4, science 4 (2 lab), social studies 3, foreign language 2 (ASL/CS counts).
    • Also: "demonstrate success in the most rigorous curriculum available at your high school"; recalculates a weighted 4.0 with +0.5 for AP, IB, Dual Enrollment, AS & A Levels; weighs "the major you put on your application and evidence of your interest in this major"; AI is permitted to "brainstorm, edit, and refine your ideas." No calculus requirement is stated.
    • Read: residency is the problem. VI-rated residency plus an explicit Georgia-majority goal means his ~8% OOS odds are structural, not stats-driven. His 10 AP exams (runchen-ap-exam-scores-2026) feed the +0.5 recalculation well, and "most rigorous curriculum available at your high school" is the phrasing that protects the Advanced-Calculus-instead-of-BC issue. See georgia-institute-of-technology-college-research.

    UPenn (Hi Reach, Rank 1.5)¶

    • Unverified. Penn publishes its CDS only via Box share links (2024-25 newest, not machine-fetchable), and every Penn Engineering admissions URL redirects to enrolled-student pages. Penn's C7 weighting and SEAS-specific criteria are not verified — do not assume. See university-of-pennsylvania-college-research.

    Harvey Mudd (Reach, Rank 1)¶

    • C7: VI = rigor, GPA, recommendations. Important = essay, extracurriculars, character. Test scores "Considered." Level of interest Not Considered.
    • Read: Mudd is the tightest "transcript + teachers" school on the list — only three VI factors, one of which is recommendations. Also answers a standing question in harvey-mudd-college-college-research: since interest is Not Considered, campus-visit effort is wasted there; the ED decision is a commitment question, not a signaling one.

    Tufts — Engineering (Reach, Rank 2)¶

    • C7: VI = rigor, class rank, GPA, essay, recommendations, character. Extracurriculars and talent "Important." Test scores "Considered." Level of interest "Considered" — confirms the list's "Dem interest: Considered" note.
    • University: holistic across "transcripts, extracurricular and community engagement, essays, recommendation letters, personal attributes and interests," testing "(when submitted)"; three application platforms with "no preference among application options"; "candidates are only considered for admission to the School to which they apply"; community descriptors "Intellectually Playful. Kind. Collaborative. Civically Engaged. Globally Minded." ED I Nov 2 2026 / ED II Jan 4 2027 / RD Jan 4 2027.
    • Engineering: no engineering-specific admission criteria are published. engineering.tufts.edu/admissions returns graduate bioengineering requirements — wrong page, don't rely on it.
    • Read: six VI factors including class rank — Tufts is the most transcript-and-recs-driven of his reaches, and applying to the School of Engineering is irrevocable. See tufts-university-college-research.

    USC — Engineering (Reach, Rank 3, drop candidate)¶

    • "A grade of C or better in at least three years of high school mathematics, including Advanced Algebra (Algebra II)." "Careful attention is paid to preparation for the intended major."
    • "USC does not conduct admission interviews, nor do we track any form of demonstrated interest in our process." Need-blind.
    • No Viterbi-specific criteria published. CDS not obtained. See university-of-southern-california-college-research.

    Case Western Reserve (Target, Rank 1)¶

    • C7: VI = rigor, class rank, GPA, extracurriculars. Test scores "Considered"; level of interest "Considered."
    • C8A is stated for Fall 2027 — his exact cycle: "Not required for admission, but considered if submitted," scores by Jan 15. This is the only school on the list whose CDS already names his cycle.
    • C5: 16 units total — English 4, math 3 req/4 rec, science 3 (2 lab req/3 rec), foreign language 2/3, social studies 2/3. C4: "Neither require nor recommend" a college-prep program.
    • University: "no minimum GPA or test score"; seeks "intellectual curiosity, initiative and a clear sense of how they'll contribute to the campus community." Requires a counselor rec plus two teacher recs.
    • Engineering: no engineering-specific coursework published.
    • Read: extracurriculars at VI is unusual for a target school and suits his VEX record; submitting the 1550 is worth it since it's "considered if submitted." See case-western-reserve-university-college-research.

    University of Washington — CSE/ECE (Target, Rank 1.5)¶

    • C7: VI = rigor, GPA, essay, state residency. Recommendations = Not Considered. Test scores = Not Considered (test-blind confirmed). Character only "Considered." Level of interest Not Considered. C6 shows "selective admission to some programs" checked — the Allen School bottleneck, formalized.
    • Read: as a WA resident he gets a VI-rated factor for free. The flip side: no recommender can help him here and the 1550 is invisible — the personal essay carries everything discretionary. Reinforces the Direct-to-College/ECE strategy in university-of-washington-college-research.

    Cal Poly SLO — Engineering (Target, Rank 2)¶

    • "We evaluate and admit students by their major. Admission is competitive in all majors"; "applicants compete against other applicants within the same major."
    • Only 9th–11th grade weighted GPA counts (senior year excluded); extra weight capped at 8 semesters.
    • No engineering-specific criteria published beyond the MCA. CDS host unreachable (DNS).
    • Read: the senior-year exclusion is the sharpest thing here — his Advanced Calculus is a senior course, so it does not raise his Cal Poly GPA at all, and whether non-AP Advanced Calculus earns MCA weight in grades 9–11 terms is still the open question flagged in cal-poly-slo-college-research.

    Santa Clara — Engineering (Target, Rank 4, drop candidate)¶

    • For engineering/science/business: "readiness at the level of calculus is expected"; 3 yrs math required, 4 recommended.
    • "Academic readiness for the program of interest will be gauged based on your expressed interest." Demonstrated interest is Important per the list.
    • CDS not obtained. See santa-clara-university-college-research.

    Boston University (Target, Rank 4, drop candidate)¶

    • College of Engineering: applicants "are required to have a year of calculus."
    • CDS not obtained (oir.bu.edu 404/403). Merit deadline Dec 1; Kilachand Honors essay. See boston-university-college-research.

    RPI (Likely, Rank 1)¶

    • "We're looking for creative, collaborative risk-takers to help address the challenges facing our world" / "Come build with us." Committee is "looking for unique strengths, leadership, and talents."
    • Test optional for fall 2027 entry. One recommendation, "preferably a math or science teacher who knows you well." Personal statement suffices — no engineering supplement. "Does not conduct interviews as part of the application process." Application fee waived if submitted by Dec 1.
    • CDS not obtained. See rensselaer-polytechnic-institute-college-research.

    WPI (Likely, Rank 1)¶

    • The thinnest published criteria on the entire list: only "test-optional institution" and "Tell us who you are and what makes you a fit for WPI." No review criteria, no engineering coursework page; CDS is 403-blocked. Effectively unverified. See worcester-polytechnic-institute-college-research.

    RIT (Likely, Rank 4, drop candidate)¶

    • C7: VI = rigor, GPA. Important = class rank, standardized test scores, essay, recommendations, character. Considered = interview, extracurriculars, talent, first gen, volunteer, work, level of applicant's interest. Not Considered = alumni, geographical residence, state residency.
    • Kate Gleason College of Engineering: 4 yrs math incl. algebra, geometry, algebra 2/trig, pre-calculus, calculus preferred; chemistry AND physics both required; Biomedical Engineering additionally requires biology. Non-engineering computing programs: 4 yrs math incl. pre-calc.
    • RIT seeks applicants "who demonstrate character, resilience, and problem-solving skills"; test optional and superscored; without scores "more weight will be placed upon the overall grade performance." One essay; no supplement unless Honors.
    • Read: RIT rates test scores "Important" — higher than any other Likely/Target school — so his superscored 1550 does real work here. BME requires high-school biology: check his transcript (runchen-hs-transcript-july-2026-final-g11) before listing BME. Note the earlier C7 read of "state residency" as VI was wrong — RIT is private and marks residency Not Considered. See rochester-institute-of-technology-college-research.

    University of Michigan (To place)¶

    • Completely unverified. Michigan returns HTTP 403 on every institutional-research and admissions host tried (ro.umich.edu, obp.umich.edu, lsa.umich.edu), across two sessions. Its C7 weighting and any College of Engineering criteria must be checked manually in a browser — same finding as the essay research. See university-of-michigan-college-research.

    Olin College of Engineering (recommended addition)¶

    • "We practice a holistic admission process, reviewing applications for both academic achievement and potential." "We look for high achievers who have demonstrated course rigor, academic mastery and strong test scores."
    • Traits named: "creativity, initiative, empathy, entrepreneurship, teamwork, passion, integrity and determination"; "We are looking for bold, entrepreneurial risk-takers!" Five "who thrives" qualities: curious, creative, collaborative, consequential, courageous.
    • Advises "a solid base in calculus and physics." Demonstrated interest matters considerably; Candidates' Weekend is mandatory and "provides the basis for final admission decisions." Deadline December 15, 2026 for the Class of 2031.
    • Read: the only school that explicitly names "strong test scores" in its prose, and the only one where a live collaborative evaluation decides the outcome. See additional-school-candidates-for-runchen-gap-analysis-2026.

    Rice University (recommended addition)¶

    • C7: all six academic factors are "Very Important," including standardized test scores — plus extracurriculars, talent, character. Level of interest "Considered." C8A: SAT/ACT "Recommended" for Fall 2026.
    • Rice "seeks students who demonstrate intellectual vitality through their course selection and their grade performance." Teacher letters "preferably with one connected to the student's intended field." "The Box" is explicitly "not used for evaluative purposes." Need-blind.
    • Read: the strongest stats-leverage school on the list. If his 1550 and 3.982 are the best parts of his file, Rice is where they count most.

    Johns Hopkins / Rose-Hulman (unresearched candidates)¶

    • JHU: all institutional-research hosts 403-blocked; no criteria verified. Rose-Hulman: site returned 404s. Both still need a first pass.

    What this changes for Runchen¶

    1. Aim the teacher recs at the schools that count them. Cornell, Rice, Tufts, and Harvey Mudd all rate recommendations "Very Important"; Cornell Engineering requires a math/science/CS teacher letter and Rice prefers one in-field. One strong math or physics teacher covers all four. UW and UCLA won't read them at all.
    2. Submit the 1550 everywhere it is read. Required at MIT, Cornell Engineering, and CMU SCS; "Very Important" at Rice; "Important" at RIT and MIT. Ignored at UW and all four UCs. There is no school on the list where submitting hurts.
    3. Character and personal qualities is the most common top-tier factor — VI at MIT, Cornell, Rice, CMU, Tufts, and Georgia Tech. That is six of his seven hardest schools, and it is decided almost entirely by the essays and recs, not the transcript.
    4. Volunteer/service is his one visible gap against a stated VI factor (CMU). Worth a deliberate look before senior year.
    5. Confirm the Advanced Calculus framing in every engineering file. BU requires a year of calculus; Santa Clara expects calculus readiness; RIT and CMU prefer it; Olin advises it. He has it, under a non-AP name, in senior year — which also means it does not count toward Cal Poly's 9th–11th GPA. The counselor's course-availability statement is load-bearing here.
    6. Reconsider Georgia Tech's Rank 1. VI-rated residency plus an explicit Georgia-majority target is a headwind no part of his profile can offset. Rice — where every academic factor is VI and his stats sit mid-band — is the better use of a Hi Reach slot, which is the same conclusion the gap analysis reached on fit grounds.
    7. Stop spending effort on demonstrated interest except at Tufts, Rice, Case Western, RPI, RIT, Santa Clara, and Olin. Everywhere else it is formally Not Considered.

    Verification status¶

    • C7 verified from the school's own CDS: MIT, Cornell (2024-25), Rice, CMU, Case Western, Tufts, Harvey Mudd, UW, Georgia Tech, RIT, UC Berkeley.
    • Prose criteria verified, no C7 obtained: USC, Santa Clara, BU, Cal Poly SLO, RPI, WPI, Olin, UCLA, UCSD, UC Davis.
    • Nothing verified: Michigan (403 on every host), UPenn (Box-only CDS, all SEAS URLs dead-end), JHU (403), Rose-Hulman (404s).
    • Method note: CDS PDFs cannot be read by fetching alone — the C7 checkboxes are AcroForm appearances, not text.
