    UC Berkeley College Research

    The University of California, Berkeley is a large public flagship (~33,000 undergrads, fall 2024) in Berkeley, CA (SF Bay Area) — the top-ranked public engineering school in the country, and a Hi Reach on Runchen's list. As a Washington resident he applies non-resident, which stacks a higher admission bar and zero financial aid on top of an already brutal rate. Because UC is test-blind, his 1550 SAT counts for nothing here — see runchen-uc-gpa-and-uc-campus-analysis for how he looks under UC's own recalculated GPA (capped-weighted ~4.29). See also the profile and preferences; it belongs to the same large-public-flagship cluster as Michigan.

    Links: admissions.berkeley.edu · engineering.berkeley.edu/admissions · bioeng.berkeley.edu · apply.universityofcalifornia.edu

    At a glance (2025-26 cycle; verify for his cycle)¶

    Field Value
    Total undergrad ~33,000 (fall 2024)
    Engineering college undergrad ~3,500–3,700 (College of Engineering; verify)
    Overall admit rate 11% (Fall 2025: 14,502 admits / 126,843 apps)
    Engineering/major admit rate Not officially published. Effectively low single digits, worse for non-residents; EECS is the single most impacted (est. ~4–8%). Major-specific bar >> campus 11%
    SAT middle 50% N/A — UC is test-blind (scores not read at all)
    Testing policy Test-blind / "Test-Free" — no SAT/ACT considered
    Admission type Apply to a specific major within the College of Engineering (or "Engineering Undeclared"); holistic review; switching into Engineering/EECS later is extremely difficult
    Application platform UC Application (NOT Common App) — one app serves all UC campuses
    App plans & deadlines No EA/ED. Opens Aug 1; file Oct 1 – Nov 30; hard deadline Nov 30. Single RD-style round
    Essays 4 Personal Insight Questions (PIQs), 350 words each, chosen from 8 prompts
    Cost (non-resident) Base tuition/fees ~$18,214 + Nonresident Supplemental Tuition ~$39,270 = ~$57,500 tuition/fees; total COA ~$82–85k/yr (verify exact). No need or merit aid for non-residents — full sticker for the family

    Fit for Runchen — the strong side¶

    • Best-in-class engineering brand. Berkeley Engineering is consistently a top-3 program nationally; the degree carries anywhere. His 7-year VEX Worlds spike (runchen-robotics-competition-journey) and hands-on designer/builder/programmer profile are exactly the kind of maker story Berkeley values.
    • Real medical-engineering pathway exists. The Bioengineering major (jointly strong with UCSF) covers biomedical devices, bioinstrumentation, imaging, and computational bio — a legitimate home for his "medical applications of engineering, needs electricity" interest. EECS is the most electricity-heavy route and pairs naturally with robotics/medical-device work.
    • Bay Area = robotics + medical-device ecosystem. Unmatched internship/research access (robotics labs, med-device startups, tech). Warm-ish, dry Mediterranean climate directly satisfies his dislike of cold weather — a clear plus over the Michigan/Case/WPI cluster.
    • Cheap to attempt. One UC Application already covers his other UC campuses; adding Berkeley costs only the ~$80 per-campus fee.

    Fit for Runchen — the concerns (weigh seriously)¶

    • Admission odds are the worst on his list for a non-resident. 11% overall is the campus rate; Engineering/EECS for an out-of-state applicant is effectively low single digits. This is a lottery ticket, not a plan.
    • Test-blind erases his single strongest asset. The 1550 SAT is invisible; everything rests on GPA (UC capped ~4.29 — solid but below the ~4.3–4.5 median for admitted Berkeley engineers), the 4 PIQs, and the robotics/medical narrative.
    • Math rigor holds up. Bellevue HS offers no AP Calculus BC; his senior course is Advanced Calculus (MAH802), post-BC and multivariable (bellevue-school-district-math-pathway-and-advanced-calculus). UC's comprehensive review explicitly weighs courses taken "in the context of what is available" at the school, so this reads as maximum rigor. Note the Bellevue College credits are UC-transferable coursework, which helps here more than at private peers.
    • Size + culture cut against his preferences. ~33,000 undergrads is the opposite of his "under-10k, campus-centered, won't-feel-lost" ideal; Berkeley also skews academically intense/competitive in impacted majors rather than clearly collaborative.
    • Cost with zero non-resident aid. ~$82–85k/yr full sticker, same hard constraint flagged for Michigan and the other UCs.
    • Locked-in major. Applying into Engineering (esp. EECS) is committing to the hardest admit bucket, with little realistic ability to transfer in later.

    Placement recommendation¶

    • Category: Reach (bordering on lottery) — for HIM specifically as a non-resident into impacted Engineering, materially harder than the headline 11%.
    • Rank (his 1–4 scale): Program-prestige view a clear 1 (elite engineering, warm climate, top BioE/EECS). On his own environment-fit view (huge size, intense/competitive vibe, anonymity worry) it reads more like a 2–3 — the same profile-vs-prestige tension noted for UCSD, Michigan, and Georgia Tech.
    • Practical notes: Uses the UC Application, not Common App; must declare a specific engineering major; write the 4 PIQs to carry the whole case (VEX Worlds + medical-engineering pivot) since the SAT is unusable. Treat as a low-cost lottery add alongside UCLA, with Davis/UCSD as the realistic UC targets.
