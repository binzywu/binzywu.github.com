    Runchen College Counseling — Working School List & Student Profile

    Student Profile¶

    • Interests: Robotics (building/designing), with a growing interest in medical applications of engineering (medical devices, drug delivery, "needs to involve electricity"); potential research/lab path over industry
    • Personality: Collaborative over competitive; avoids environments where he must "stand out" to win; highly relational — wants good professors, good peers, and human connection as the core of college experience
    • Style: Mild, independent fashion sense emerging; wants autonomy in decisions but historically deferred to parents — family actively working to change dynamic
    • Academic: Handles workload without visible strain; not a grind-for-top-grades type; likely middle of cohort by choice, not ability
    • Social anxiety: Worried about how to make friends in large, anonymous lecture-hall environments; more comfortable in smaller, campus-centered schools

    Working School List (6 schools discussed)¶

    School Tier Notes
    Carnegie Mellon Reach Strong robotics (CS school, very competitive); engineering school slightly easier entry; diverse campus (arts + engineering tempers intensity); Pittsburgh has good collaborative feel vs. MIT
    Harvey Mudd Reach Part of 5-school Claremont Consortium; liberal-arts-for-STEM model; must articulate fit with breadth philosophy in essays; Claremont is upscale residential, easy CA airport access
    Case Western Target Pre-med/engineering intersection; similar Carnegie Mellon energy; adjacent to medical center; Cleveland is revitalized city; 5-year BS/MS option noted
    Tufts Target Engineering school within liberal arts university; only ~8 liberal arts requirements; Boston location; ~7,600 undergrad; apply directly to engineering program
    WPI (Worcester) Safety First robotics program in US; VEX Robotics sponsor (family already knows name); train line to Boston; comparable tier to RPI/RIT but better location access
    Santa Clara Safety Proximity to Silicon Valley; strong industry connections/investment; balanced campus culture; good safety if UW not chosen

    Additional Schools Mentioned for Future List¶

    • UW (baseline in-state comparison)
    • University of Michigan (parents positive; good robotics)
    • USC (private; just added ED for business school; EA also available; AI school expansion may open seats)
    • Georgia Tech (aerospace interest; apply-and-see safety/target)
    • State schools (UI, Purdue, Florida) noted as common applicants who rarely enroll — deprioritized this round

    Counselor Observations¶

    • Private schools preferred over state schools for Runchen: less capacity-constraint pressure on major declaration, more collaborative culture, smaller cohorts
    • Ideal school size: under ~10,000 undergrad with central walkable campus (Cornell aesthetic resonated with him)
    • Schools under 10k, campus-centered, with smaller class sizes better fit his friend-making concerns
    • CMU ED question deferred to Aug/Sep strategy session — don't cross that bridge yet

    Process & Next Steps¶

    • Parent questionnaire due June 17 (in counselor's Google Drive → Wu family → college selection → application information → recommendations & activities)
    • Essay intro session: Evening of June 18 or June 19 (Juneteenth holiday — Runchen off school)
    • Essay workshop: ~5 sessions, June 19 – ~July 7, twice weekly; speed-writing exercises, not full drafts
    • Runchen departs for summer program: ~Friday night July 10
    • List-building approach: Counselor will propose ~12 schools; Runchen selects 6 to research deeply — mirrors approach used successfully with counselor's own son
    • Action for parents: Email counselor a simple list of any additional schools to add to the mix (USC, Michigan, Georgia Tech, etc.) — no reasons needed, just names

    Key Insight on Student Autonomy¶

    Runchen has historically deferred decisions back to parents; family is deliberately shifting to give him ownership. Counselor will frame school research as coming from the student. When it comes to final decision, plan to give him two finalists and two weeks — same method used with counselor's son Piers.
