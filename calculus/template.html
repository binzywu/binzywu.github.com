How to study the new units

  • Parametric / polar: track the parameter before eliminating it; draw the tracing direction and verify the area bounds.
  • Vectors: draw the geometry, calculate, then check whether the answer should be a scalar, vector, distance or area.
  • Curves: keep position, velocity, speed and acceleration distinct.
  • Multivariable: state what is held fixed; check domains, paths and differentiability assumptions.
  • Optimization: list every candidate and inspect boundaries; the gradient equations alone are not a complete solution.

Use one lesson per session, then redo its paper problem without notes a day later. Choose the next lesson using the teacher’s current assignment.

A quick series-test decision guide

  1. Terms do not go to zero? Diverges.
  2. Geometric, telescoping or p-series? Use the known form; write a finite partial sum for telescoping.
  3. Positive rational/algebraic terms? Comparison or limit comparison.
  4. Factorials/exponentials? Ratio test; nth powers often suggest root test.
  5. Alternating? Check absolute convergence, then monotone magnitudes tending to zero.
  6. Power series? Radius first; endpoints separately.

Not an exhaustive algorithm. If a test is inconclusive, change tests rather than forcing a conclusion.
