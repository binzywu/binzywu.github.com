01 · Terms shrink. What happens to the total?

Predict first: will the running total settle? Compare the left chart (individual terms) with the right chart (their sum). A finite plot can suggest behavior; the named convergence test is what justifies it.

Running totals Sₙ

Both x-axes count terms. Each y-axis rescales to its own values; read the tick labels before comparing heights.

02 · A polynomial shadows a function

All polynomials here are centered at 0. Increase the degree, then move the evaluation point away from the center. Compare the measured error with a rigorous Lagrange bound.

Solid blue: original function · dashed orange: Taylor polynomial · vertical gray line: selected x. The plot shows x from −3 to 3.

For sin and cos, M = 1 is valid. For eˣ between 0 and x, M = exp(max(0, x)). The bound uses degree n even when a coefficient vanishes. At high degrees, very small differences may reach floating-point precision.

03 · Freeze one variable to see a partial derivative

On z = x² + xy + y², a partial derivative is the slope of a one-variable slice. Choose a point (a, b), choose which variable moves, and compare the slice with its tangent line.

Solid blue: the slice through the surface · dashed orange: its tangent line · vertical gray line: point of tangency.

04 · Position, velocity and the shape of a curve

Choose a curve and move the parameter t. The point follows the curve; the arrow shows the direction of nonzero velocity. On a polar curve, remember that both r and the angle change with t.

Blue: complete curve · teal point: current position · orange arrow: unit velocity direction, not speed. Axes use equal distance scales.

05 · Rotate a vector; watch its products change

Keep u = ⟨2, 0, 0⟩ fixed and rotate v in the xy-plane. Predict when the dot product is negative and when the cross product points in the negative z direction.

Blue arrow: u · orange arrow: v · dashed teal segment: perpendicular projection to the x-axis. The cross product points out of or into this plane; its signed z-component is reported below.

06 · Which direction climbs fastest?

Explore f(x, y) = x² + 2y² on a contour map. Move the point and rotate a unit direction. The dot product with the gradient gives the instantaneous rate in that direction.

Contours have heights 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 (unequal increments). Blue arrow shows 0.4∇f for scale; orange arrow is the chosen unit vector. Equal x/y distance scales.

  3. Solve the paper problem and both checks.
