    • No standalone undergraduate Robotics major.

    Likes: close to Boston's major medical and biotech ecosystem — hospitals, biotech firms, research institutes. This medical/biotech proximity aligns with his growing lean toward medical applications of engineering (see runchen-college-preferences-overall). Dislikes: classic New England-style campus architecture (a mild aesthetic note).

    Current-Cycle Research (2026-07-24)

    Fit for Runchen — the strong side

    • Direct-to-engineering admit: no internal transfer or impacted-major gauntlet after enrollment.
    • Stats are above the bar: his 1550 superscore (EBRW 760 / Math 790) sits at/above the 75th percentile; GPA 3.982 is competitive. Selectivity, not his numbers, is the constraint.
    • Genuine BME fit: full standalone Biomedical Engineering department plus Electrical & Computer Engineering — well-aligned with his "engineering that involves electricity, medical devices" interest, no forced wet-lab/biology track.
    • Size and culture: ~7,000 undergrads is squarely inside his <10k preference; collaborative, non-cutthroat, campus-centered reputation.

    Fit for Runchen — the concerns (weigh seriously)

    • No EA option: only ED (binding) gives an admit-rate boost (ED admit ~32%, 1,071/3,344). Applying RD non-bindingly means facing the ~10% pool.
    • Math ceiling is actually multivariable, not AB: Bellevue HS offers no AP Calculus BC, and he takes post-BC Advanced Calculus (MAH802) senior year (bellevue-school-district-math-pathway-and-advanced-calculus). Not a rigor gap.
    • No merit aid: affordability depends entirely on need-based aid / Tuition Pact eligibility (family income under $150k).
    • No standalone undergraduate Robotics major — robotics is pursued through ME/ECE/CS, not a dedicated degree.

    Placement recommendation

    • Category: Reach (private, residency-neutral).
    • Rank: 2 on his list. Prestige view and his own environment-fit view largely agree here — strong BME/engineering brand AND a small, collaborative, campus-centered feel he wants.
    • Practical notes: Common App with a required Tufts supplement; must choose School of Engineering on the application; the only real admit-rate lever is ED (binding).
