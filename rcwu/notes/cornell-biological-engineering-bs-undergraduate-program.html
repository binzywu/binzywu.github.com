    Cornell Biological Engineering (BS) Undergraduate Program

    Updated 2026-08-09
    college-researchcornellbioengineeringbiomedicalengineeringabetrunchencurriculummedical-devices

    Cornell's Biological Engineering (BE, BS) major, administered by the Dept. of Biological & Environmental Engineering (BEE) and offered jointly through the Duffield College of Engineering and the College of Agriculture & Life Sciences (CALS). ABET-accredited. Relevant to cornell-university-college-research and Runchen's robotics-to-medical-devices pivot — see runchen-robotics-competition-journey and the parallel UCSD Bioengineering note. Cornell is a list school; essay angles in Cornell Sample Essays Reference (Fall 2027).

    ⚠️ Important: this is NOT Cornell's Biomedical Engineering major

    Cornell runs two distinct majors and the difference matters for Runchen:

    • Biological Engineering (BE) — this note. Focus is biological + environmental systems: food supply, natural-resource remediation (water/soil/air/energy), plus bioprocessing, biomaterials, bioinstrumentation, and cellular/molecular bioengineering. Grew out of ag/environmental engineering.
    • Biomedical Engineering (BME) — a separate Cornell BS major (own affiliation track, courses BME 2000/2010/4190/4390 etc.). This is the closer fit for a student pursuing medical devices / bioelectricity, especially the Circuits, Signals and Sensors: Instrumentation Laboratory (BME 4390) course. Recommend a dedicated note on Cornell BME as the primary target; BE is the biology/environment-leaning cousin.

    Where BE does touch his interest: BEE 4500 Bioinstrumentation (required), and BE students can take the ENGRD electrical-sciences distribution (e.g. ENGRD 2100 Intro to Circuits). But the core BE identity is bio/environmental, not device/electrical.

    Structure & requirements

    • Direct admit: first-year and transfer students are admitted directly into the major; most prereqs met by admission requirements. (Contrast with the internal Duffield affiliation process below.)
    • Minimum 128 credits for the degree; in-person, Ithaca NY.
    • Math/science core: MATH 1910/1920/2930/2940 (must earn C− or better in each), PHYS 1112/1110/2213, CHEM 2090/2091, organic chem, CS 1112/1110.
    • Biological sciences: ≥15 credits — two intro-bio sequences + lab, biochemistry, and a 2000+ bio elective. (Bio-heavy relative to most engineering majors.)
    • Major-required core (48 cr): ENGRI intro, ENGRD 2020 Statics & Mechanics of Solids, thermodynamics, BEE 2600 Principles of Biological Engineering, BEE 3310 Bio-Fluid Mechanics, BEE 3400 Biomaterials, BEE 3500 Heat & Mass Transfer, BEE 3600 Molecular & Cellular Bioengineering, BEE 4500 Bioinstrumentation, and uncertainty analysis/statistics.
    • Focus-area electives (15 cr, ≥6 from one area) across five tracks: (1) Ecological & Agricultural Systems, (2) Biomechanics & Biotransport, (3) Biomaterials Engineering, (4) Cellular & Molecular Biotechnology, (5) Modeling & Computation in Bioengineering.
    • Capstone design project required of all students; emphasis throughout on communication + teamwork.
    • Grades: all major requirements letter-graded; only one D allowed in the major (excluding the math sequence, which needs C−+). Semester & cumulative GPA ≥ 2.0.
    • Honors: +9 credits (research/thesis via BEE 4990/4993, or teaching, or advanced courses) with a public presentation; apply by end of 3rd week of senior year.

    Notable / for Runchen

    • Program explicitly lists biomedical engineering among recent graduate career paths, and supports pre-med lab tracks — so BE → medical is a real (if indirect) route.
    • Students may add a minor or second engineering major — a BE + BME or BE + ECE combination could bridge the electrical/device gap, though that's a heavy load.
    • His AP credit may cover parts of intro bio/chem/calc; note the catalog warns pre-meds to take bio/chem at Cornell rather than lean on AP.
    • University-wide oddities: swim test + 2 PE courses required to graduate.
