    Cornell Bioengineering Major + Robotics Minor Pathway

    Updated 2026-08-10
    college-researchcornellbioengineeringbiomedicalroboticsengineeringrunchenmedical-devicesminor

    How to pair a Cornell bioengineering major with the Undergraduate Robotics Minor — arguably the single best on-paper vehicle for Runchen's story, welding his 7-year VEX robotics career to his robotics-to-medical-devices pivot. Connects to cornell-university-college-research, Cornell Biological Engineering (BE), and the parallel UCSD Bioengineering note.

    Which major to pair it with

    Cornell has two distinct "bio" engineering majors; both are eligible for the Robotics Minor:

    • Biological Engineering (BE) — molecular biology, ecological systems, synthetic biology, biomaterials. Fits biohybrid robots (machines powered by living tissue/organic matter) and ag automation.
    • Biomedical Engineering (BME) — human medicine, biomechanics, clinical tech. Fits robotic prosthetics, medical imaging, surgical-robot components.

    For Runchen (medical devices + "needs electricity"), BME is the more direct major, with the robotics minor supplying the hardware/controls/perception depth he already loves from VEX. (See the BE note's BE-vs-BME caveat.)

    Robotics Minor — verified requirements (2026-27 catalog)

    Eligibility: open to all undergrads except those minoring in ECE, Mechanical Engineering, or CS. Pre-approval of the minor plan is required, and each course must be completed with a grade of C or better to count.

    Structure: six distinct courses = three Fundamentals + three Specialization from a single category. (This corrects the pasted "Group A/B, three categories" framing — the catalog uses Fundamentals/Specialization and has four specialization categories.)

    Fundamentals (choose 3)

    CS 3700 Foundations of AI Reasoning & Decision-Making (or CS 3780 / ECE 4200 Machine Learning) · CS 4756 Robot Learning · INFO 4410 Re-Designing Robots · MAE 3780 Mechatronics · MAE 4180 Autonomous Mobile Robots · MAE 4190 Fast Robots · MAE 4760 Foundations of Robotics · MAE 4810 Robot Perception · MAE 5780 Feedback Control Systems.

    _For a device/prosthetics builder, MAE 3780 Mechatronics + MAE 4760 Foundations of Robotics are the natural VEX-to-Cornell hardware/controls foundation._

    Specialization (choose 3 in ONE category)

    • Intelligence: Robot Learning, ML, Deep Learning for Robotics, Autonomous Mobile Robots, Foundations of Robotics, Robot Manipulation, Human-Robot Interaction, Formal Methods.
    • Modeling, Dynamics & Control: Fast Robots, Intermediate Dynamics, Foundations of Robotics, Feedback Control Systems, Robot Manipulation, Model-Based Estimation, Multivariable Control Theory, Design & Control of Haptic Systems (MAE 6800). (Best for smooth prosthetic-limb motion control.)
    • Perception: Computer Vision, Deep Learning for Robotics, Autonomous Mobile Robots, Fast Robots, Integrated Micro Sensors & Actuators (MAE 4320), Robot Perception, Model-Based Estimation. (Best for medical imaging.)
    • Systems and Design: BEE 3900 Bio-Robotics · Re-Designing Robots · Robots in the Wild · Architectural Robotics · ECE 4760 Digital Systems Design Using Microcontrollers · ECE 5725 Embedded Operating Systems · ECE 5960 Micro and Nano Robotics · Intro to Rapid Prototyping & Physical Computing · Mechatronics · Micro Sensors & Actuators · Human-Robot Interaction · Haptic Systems.

    Key discovery the pasted guidance missed: a Biological & Environmental Engineering course, BEE 3900 Bio-Robotics, counts directly toward the minor under Systems and Design — the cleanest bio↔robotics bridge Cornell offers. The Systems & Design track also holds the most hardware/device-flavored courses (microcontrollers, micro/nano robotics, prototyping, haptics), making it the strongest specialization for a medical-device path.

    Petition option: one Specialization course may be replaced by ≥3 credits of independent research (e.g. MAE 4900) with a faculty note on the robotics aspect — a clean route to fold in lab work.

    The standout hook: biohybrid robotics research

    Cornell is world-renowned for merging biology and robotics. Undergraduate research labs to name in a "why Cornell" essay:

    • Organic Robotics Lab (ORL): soft, living, biohybrid robots — e.g. robots using living fungal (mushroom) networks to sense electrical signals, and soft prosthetic skins that sense pressure like human flesh.
    • Shepherd Laboratory: soft actuators and 3D-printed flexible/stretchable materials mimicking muscle for bio-inspired robotics. (Lab specifics not re-verified against current Cornell MAE pages — confirm before citing in an essay.)

    Why this matters for the application

    • Narrative unity: "VEX robot designer/builder/programmer → biomedical engineer building medical/surgical robots and prosthetics" is a clean, authentic throughline, and this major+minor is its concrete academic vehicle.
    • Essay + "why Cornell" material: naming the Systems & Design track, BEE 3900 Bio-Robotics, MAE 3780/4760, and ORL/Shepherd is exactly the specific, non-generic detail Cornell supplements reward — see Cornell Sample Essays Reference (Fall 2027).
    • Action items: (1) decide BE vs BME as primary major (leaning BME); (2) map the six minor courses early — bio majors are chem/bio-heavy, so technical-elective space is tight; (3) verify the ORL/Shepherd lab details before using them in a supplement.
