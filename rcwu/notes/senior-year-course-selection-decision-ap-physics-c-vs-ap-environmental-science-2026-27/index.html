    Senior Year Course Selection Decision: AP Physics C vs AP Environmental Science (2026-27)

    Decision recorded 2026-07-27 for Runchen's senior year (2026-27): if the schedule allows, replace AP Environmental Science with AP Physics C – Mechanics. Reasoning below, along with the fallback case if it can't fit.

    The proposed senior schedule¶

    As originally planned: AP Macroeconomics/Microeconomics, AP Biology, AP Environmental Science, Advanced Calculus (MAH802), AP French, Senior English, Band (Percussion Ensemble).

    After the swap: AP Macro/Micro, AP Biology, AP Physics C – Mechanics, Advanced Calculus, AP French, Senior English, Band.

    Same number of courses, same subject slot. This is a substitution, not an addition — the workload does not grow, it gets better aimed.

    Course facts (verified from the BSD 405 catalog, 2026-07-27)¶

    Field AP Physics C – Mechanics (SCA701)
    Offered at Bellevue HS, Newport HS, Sammamish HS
    Grades 11–12
    Length / credit Full year, 1.0 Lab Science credit
    Prerequisite "Calculus or concurrent" (verbatim)
    Calculus-based Yes — "Methods of calculus are used as appropriate in developing concepts and in applying them to physical problems"
    College credit Via AP exam only (no dual-enrollment partner)
    Framing "the opening portion of a college-level physics sequence intended for students planning to major in the physical sciences or engineering"; "Strong emphasis is placed on solving a variety of challenging problems in a laboratory setting"

    Important contrast with the calculus situation: unlike AP Calculus BC — which BHS does not offer at all (see bellevue-school-district-math-pathway-and-advanced-calculus) — Physics C – Mechanics IS offered at Bellevue HS. So "his school doesn't offer it" is not available as a defense here. Note AP Physics C – Electricity & Magnetism is Newport HS only, so the E&M half genuinely is unavailable to him.

    The case for the swap¶

    1. It is the single most relevant AP for an engineering applicant. Calculus-based mechanics is the first course in essentially every engineering curriculum. The catalog itself positions it as pre-engineering.
    2. It academically corroborates his spike. Torque, gear ratios, moments of inertia, center of mass — exactly what he has been doing physically for seven years in VEX. Currently his robotics record and his transcript don't reinforce each other; this makes them do so.
    3. Rigor is the last factor he can still move. Per what-each-college-on-runchen-s-list-values-most-fall-2027-cycle, "rigor of secondary school record" is Very Important at 10 of the 11 verified Common Data Sets (the exception is MIT, where character is the sole VI factor). Senior schedule is the final input under his control.
    4. Three schools name physics explicitly: Harvey Mudd requires a year each of calculus, chemistry, and physics to enroll; Olin advises "a solid base in calculus and physics"; RIT requires physics with calculus preferred.
    5. He is unusually over-prepared for the prerequisite. Most students struggle in Physics C because the calculus is new to them. He has a 5 in AP Calculus AB and will be taking multivariable Advanced Calculus concurrently. The prereq is merely "Calculus or concurrent" — he clears it by a wide margin. This is materially less risky for him than for a typical enrollee.
    6. AP Environmental Science is the weakest course on the schedule. Widely read as the least rigorous AP science, and it is occupying the science slot on an engineering application. AP Env Sci with no Physics C is the combination that invites the wrong inference. Nothing on his list requires or recommends Env Sci, and his lab-science units are covered either way (six science credits post-swap).

    Result: AP Bio + AP Physics C is close to the ideal senior science pair for "robotics plus medical engineering that involves electricity" (see runchen-college-preferences-overall).

    Courses NOT to touch, and why¶

    Course Why it stays
    Advanced Calculus (MAH802) The crown jewel of the transcript — post-BC multivariable, dual-enrolled for Bellevue College MATH& 153 + 254 (10 credits). For engineering admissions, how far you got in math is the primary rigor axis; physics depth is secondary.
    Band (Percussion Ensemble) Fourth consecutive year. It is the honest answer to Harvey Mudd's "dream HSA class" prompt and to MIT's direct question about "something you do simply for the pleasure of it" (see applicant-characteristics-each-college-expects-fall-2027-cycle). Dropping it breaks a four-year commitment and removes the only counterweight to an all-robotics record. See runchen-music-activities.
    AP Biology His only biology is an 8th-grade AL course. BME programs want it — RIT's BME requires biology outright — and it supports the medical-device angle. This is the science not to trade.
    Senior English Required for graduation.
    AP French A fourth year of world language is itself a rigor signal; cutting it to add physics is a lateral move, not a gain.

    Alternative drop if Env Sci must be kept (e.g. for a teacher recommendation relationship): AP Macro/Micro Economics. No engineering school on his list cares about it, and he is already well past every social-studies requirement with AP Modern World History, AP US History, and AP US Government.

    Honest caveats¶

    • The AP score will never appear on any application. The exam is May 2027, after all decisions are released. The entire admissions value is (a) the course title on the transcript and in the counselor's "most demanding curriculum" assessment, and (b) the senior-fall grade, which is reported — Harvey Mudd requests first-term senior grades for RD applicants, and most schools request a mid-year report. The whole bet rides on one semester.
    • Workload is the real question, not ability. Five APs plus a dual-enrolled multivariable course while writing 15+ applications between October and January. The counselor's read is that he is "not a grind-for-top-grades type," and he has a documented sensitivity to things being "too hard" (runchen-college-counseling-working-school-list-student-profile). He already has one B, in AP Literature. If a fall B in Physics C is a live possibility, that is a genuine cost — though a B in Physics C reads better to an engineering reader than an A in Environmental Science.

    If Physics C genuinely cannot fit¶

    This is a minor cost, not a big one. Assessed 2026-07-27:

    • He already holds a strong, exam-verified physics credential: AP Physics 1 with a 5, taken in 10th grade. Every physics requirement on his list is satisfied — Mudd's enrollment requirement, Olin's advice, RIT's calculus-preferred physics, and Georgia Tech's C5 (4 science units, 2 lab).
    • Not one school on the list requires or even recommends Physics C specifically. Checked against all eleven verified Common Data Sets and every engineering-school requirements page.
    • His rigor case rests on math, which is untouched. Advanced Calculus is a stronger senior signal than Physics C would be.
    • The volume is already there: eleven APs, six 5s, every score 4 or above, all-A but for one semester. A twelfth AP whose score won't exist until after decisions doesn't change that picture.

    Two things make the fallback safe: 1. Keep AP Bio. With Physics C off the table, Bio becomes the science doing the work — and it's the better one for the medical-device interest anyway. A senior year with no lab science at all is the version to avoid. 2. Let the robotics record carry the applied-physics story. Seven years of mechanical design and the VEX Worlds Builder Award demonstrate mechanics fluency that no AP score conveys.

    Bottom line: not taking Physics C will not cost him an admission. It is worth understanding why it doesn't fit, though — a period conflict is sometimes solvable with a counselor conversation, whereas genuine workload capacity is a legitimate answer that should be respected.

    Action items¶

    • Confirm with the counselor that both Advanced Calculus and AP Physics C are actually running at BHS in 2026-27. The BSD catalog states offering depends on enrollment — and the Advanced Calculus question was already open in bellevue-school-district-math-pathway-and-advanced-calculus.
    • Check whether the swap is even schedulable (period conflicts vs. true capacity limits — the answer changes the recommendation).
    • Put the decision to Runchen himself. The family is deliberately shifting course and college decisions to him, and he is the one who knows his appetite for a five-AP application-season fall.
    • Verify he is registered for the College in the High School option in Advanced Calculus — the transferable Bellevue College credits are the part that carries weight beyond the course title.
