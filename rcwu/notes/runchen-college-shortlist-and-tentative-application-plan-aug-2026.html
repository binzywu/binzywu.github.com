    Why each school is on the list

    The sheet's rationale column is now mostly about advising, support structure, and program fit rather than prestige:

    • Cornell Engineering — his favorite on the visit; strongest engineering in the Ivy League; high rigor; student-led project teams; faculty advisors assigned from the start; engineering co-op available.
    • UPenn — good experience at ESAP with state-of-the-art tools; smaller engineering school, so more supportive than Cornell by virtue of size; full professors teach the classes; dedicated Penn Engineering advising.
    • Harvey Mudd — broad, deep common core across all STEM; faculty-organized projects from freshman year leading to the senior industry Clinic; tight advising; general engineering major. Essays matter unusually much here — Mudd screens for program understanding and fit.
    • Tufts Engineering — small, intimate engineering school inside a private university; dedicated School of Engineering advising team with an advisor for every student; near Boston's biotech and tech hubs.
    • USC Viterbi — first year Viterbi offers ED, so no track record, but likely still easier than Cornell, Penn, or Mudd. His SAT is at the 75th percentile and robotics is a strong hook. Would convert to a Target if he applies ED. Engineering Freshman Academy elective plus assigned advisors; BS BMEN (biomedical with electrical emphasis) matches his stated interest.
    • Case Western — good engineering with very strong biotech, anchored by the medical school; the best match on the list for the biotech interest.
    • UC San Diego — college system makes a large UC feel more community-oriented (note: general-ed requirements differ by college). Excellent life sciences for bioengineering. Caveat: most engineering majors are capped and must be declared at application — if he doesn't get the major, he may not be able to switch. Poor fit if he wants to explore after admission.
    • U Washington — in-state tuition and genuine resident leverage. Apply Direct-to-College with ECE as first choice, not CSE (resolved in University of Washington College Research): ECE's Neurotechnology Pathway + Center for Neurotechnology is the exact "medical engineering that involves electricity" fit, and listing ECE lands him in the general DTC pool (~39.5%, Target) rather than the brutally selective Allen School CSE Direct-to-Major pool (a Reach even for a strong resident). BioE is the wrong door here (no device/bioelectronics track). Caveat: ECE is oversubscribed at the year-1 placement gate, so first-choice placement isn't guaranteed even after admission.
    • Santa Clara — in Silicon Valley with the connections that implies; strong job placement and internships; top-tier mid-career salaries; supportive advising at mid size.
    • WPI — established Robotics Engineering program; an hour from Cambridge/Boston biotech; required hands-on projects provide built-in scaffolding. His robotics background is the most natural narrative fit of any school on the list.
    • Northeastern (added 2026-08-21) — co-op powerhouse in the hands-on lane (like WPI, bigger name); Boston biotech. Has a declarable robotics minor (ECE-run, open to all students) a bioengineering major can add — one of only ~5 schools researched where the "BME + robotics minor" combo actually exists. No supplement essay; 1 counselor + 1 teacher rec. Caveat: ~5.6% admit rate makes it a genuine Reach, not a Target.
    • Rice (added 2026-08-21) — small, collaborative, residential-college system (strongest "won't feel lost" fit on the list); declare-later (non-binding major selection). Strong BME. No robotics minor — robotics only via electives (ELEC 498/450, MECH 488), so don't build the essays around a minor. Supplement: academic-interests 150w + why-Rice 150w + community 500w; 1 counselor + 2 teacher recs (one in intended field recommended). 8% admit → Reach.
    • Georgia Tech (added 2026-08-21) — top-tier BME (Coulter, joint with Emory) and a robotics minor that explicitly lists BME as eligible — a true "BME + robotics minor" school. Supplement eliminated this cycle (Common App essay only) and recs optional/not considered → low-effort application. Caveat: direct-to-major and capped; as an out-of-state applicant the real BME bar sits above the ~10% OOS headline rate.

    Considered and declined

    • Cal Poly SLO (reconsidered 2026-08-21, kept dropped) — despite a near-zero-effort application (no essays/recs, single Nov 30 deadline) and the best "Learn by Doing" hands-on culture fit on the list, it's redundant with Santa Clara (warm CA, private, uses his SAT, gives merit aid) and WPI (project-based), both already Likely. Decisive negatives: test-blind (his 1550 SAT and robotics spike count for nothing — admission is the GPA/rigor MCA formula), ~$46–49k OOS with essentially no CSU aid (UW is his cheap in-state anchor instead), and ~22k undergrad vs his <10k preference. See Cal Poly SLO College Research.

    Open decisions

    1. Where the single ED goes. Cornell, Penn, Mudd (ED I or II), Tufts (ED II), and USC are all marked with a tentative ED — only one can be used. Cornell vs. Penn is the live question, with Penn flagged as the more supportive environment and Cornell as the visit favorite.
    2. USC ED vs EA. ED converts USC into a Target; EA keeps optionality. Both are 11/01.
    3. UC breadth. Applying to UC San Diego makes additional UC campuses nearly free in effort (~$80–85/campus fee) — see runchen-college-ranking-rubric-and-strategy-2026.
