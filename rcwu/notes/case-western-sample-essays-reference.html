    Strategic Approach for CWRU with No Supplement

    The challenge

    Most schools with strong engineering programs ask "Why this major?" and "Why this school?" separately. CWRU does not — they rely entirely on the Common App essay to learn about the applicant's motivations and fit.

    Two strategic options:

    Option 1: Major/field-focused Common App essay that embeds CWRU fit

    Use a Common App prompt that allows weaving in CWRU-specific reasons while telling the robotics-to-medical-devices story.

    Best Common App prompts for this approach:

    • Prompt 4: "Reflect on something that someone has done for you that has made you happy or thankful in a surprising way. How has this gratitude affected or motivated you?" — could frame around a VEX teammate, mentor, or family member who sparked the medical-devices pivot
    • Prompt 5: "Discuss an accomplishment, event, or realization that sparked a period of personal growth and a new understanding of yourself or others." — the VEX Worlds win or a design failure that led to the biomedical pivot
    • Prompt 6: "Describe a topic, idea, or concept you find so engaging that it makes you lose all track of time. Why does it captivate you? What or who do you turn to when you want to learn more?" — robotics design/electrical systems/medical devices

    How to embed CWRU fit: In the final paragraph or throughout, mention specific CWRU resources (Biomedical Engineering department, proximity to Cleveland Clinic and University Hospitals, Human Fusions Institute, collaborative research culture) as the natural next step after the growth/realization described.

    Option 2: Identity/values-focused Common App essay, rely on application context for fit

    Use the Common App essay to show character, values, and personality (the "shy but competitive," "collaborative designer," "immigrant-builder" dimensions), and let the activities list + intended major + any interview convey the CWRU-specific fit.

    Best Common App prompts for this approach:

    • Prompt 1: "Some students have a background, identity, interest, or talent that is so meaningful they believe their application would be incomplete without it." — immigrant story, bilingual identity, or 7-year robotics as core identity
    • Prompt 2: "The lessons we take from obstacles we encounter can be fundamental to later success. Recount a time when you faced a challenge, setback, or failure. How did it affect you, and what did you learn from the experience?" — VEX design failure, competition loss, or team conflict
    • Prompt 7 (open topic): Full creative freedom to tell any story

    Trade-off: This approach is safer for reusing the essay across all 17+ Common App schools on Runchen's list, but may feel less tailored to CWRU's medical-engineering strengths.
