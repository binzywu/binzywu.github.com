    University of Wisconsin-Madison College Research

    Updated 2026-07-25
    college-researchuniversity-of-wisconsin-madisonengineeringbiomedical-engineeringelectrical-engineeringrunchen

    University of Wisconsin–Madison is a large public flagship (37,198 undergrad, Fall 2025) in Madison, WI — not currently on the college list; this note evaluates whether it should be. The short version: a genuinely strong academic fit on the medical-device axis, undermined by the same three objections that already dog Michigan and UIUC — very large, very cold, and expensive with no residency advantage. See the profile and preferences. Closest comparisons on his list: Michigan and UIUC (same big-public-OOS profile), and Case Western (the BME-fit benchmark he liked).

    Links: admissions.wisc.edu · engineering.wisc.edu (College of Engineering) · engineering.wisc.edu/programs/degrees/biomedical-engineering-bs/

    Fit for Runchen — the strong side

    • BME here is the best structural match for his interests of any big public on the list. One of its four specializations is explicitly "Bioinstrumentation and medical devices" — "the application of electronics, measurement principles, and techniques to develop devices used in diagnosis and treatment of disease," with named examples including brain–computer interfaces, implantable electrodes, sensors, and tumor ablation, plus a neuroengineering subfield covering implantable neuroprosthetics. This is almost a verbatim match for his stated interest: medical engineering that "needs to involve electricity" and robots that "operate on people" (runchen-college-preferences-overall). Materially more targeted than UIUC's Bioengineering, which has no medical-device track.
    • Design-first curriculum, every semester — this is the standout feature. "Our undergraduate program was founded with engineering design as the backbone of the curriculum." Students build for real clients (faculty, people with biomedical challenges, industry sponsors) each semester via the BME 200 → 201 → 300 → 400/402 capstone sequence. For a 7-year VEX designer-builder-programmer (runchen-robotics-competition-journey), this is the closest thing on his list to a continuous hands-on build track — arguably a better fit for how he actually works than a theory-forward program.
    • Direct admit to the major, no second admissions gate. Unlike UW Seattle's Direct-to-College (admit → ENGRUD → compete for placement), he'd be admitted straight into BME or ECE. One gate, not two.
    • Non-binding EA with a January 31 answer. A genuinely useful list-shaping tool: an early, non-restrictive admit that doesn't consume his single ED card (which is likely going to Cornell).
    • Industry pipeline is real: BME employers named include Medtronic, Boston Scientific, Abbott, Epic, Exact Sciences — a medical-device corridor, not a generic-engineering list. $77k first-year median.
    • ~41% admit rate makes this a true Target, likely the safest strong-BME option on his list. His 3.982 GPA / 10 APs / all-A record maps directly onto what UW says it reads: "top grades—mostly As—in challenging courses."

    Fit for Runchen — the concerns (weigh seriously)

    • 37,198 undergrad is the largest school he'd be considering — bigger than Michigan (~35k), UW Seattle (~35k), and UIUC (~37k). This runs straight into his "<10k, campus-centered, won't-get-lost" criterion, the single objection that has downgraded every big public on his list. He has no Rank-1 "won't feel lost" school above ~20k undergrad.
    • Madison winters are worse than anything else on his list. Cold is a recurring, self-reported problem — "It was cold at Cornell and I got sick... Tends to get colds and allergies a lot in the winter" — and Madison is colder than Ithaca, Ann Arbor, Cleveland, and Worcester. This is arguably the worst weather fit on the entire list.
    • No residency advantage and a hard cost profile. ~$62–66k/yr nonresident with no in-state break. Wisconsin now guarantees admission to eligible EA residents, which structurally tightens the nonresident pool. Compare UW Seattle at ~$32–35k with a resident preference — Madison costs roughly double for a school he'd likely rank lower on fit.
    • The progression gate is a real second hurdle, just relocated. Direct admit is not the end: he must clear math through Math 222, four foundational courses, and per-major GPA minimums in two semesters — and Regulation 7 explicitly caps progression at program capacity. So the "one gate, not two" advantage over UW Seattle is weaker than it first appears; the risk moves from admission to progression.
    • No published class profile. UW publishes neither SAT ranges nor a resident/nonresident admit split, and states "we don't use formulas or charts." The ~41% rate is a blended figure — his actual nonresident odds are unverified, though almost certainly still Target territory.
    • It adds nothing his list lacks strategically. He already has Michigan and UIUC as big-public OOS reaches and Case Western as a Rank-1 BME target he actively liked. Wisconsin is a fourth entry in a category he's already covered and consistently ranks low on fit.

    Placement recommendation

    • Category: Target (~41% admit; his profile sits comfortably above the middle of the pool).
    • Rank (his 1–4 scale): likely a 3–4. Program quality argues 2; his own environment criteria — huge, cold, expensive, Midwest — argue 4. The same profile-vs-prestige tension as UIUC and Michigan, except Wisconsin's prestige side is weaker than either while its fit side is no better.
    • Verdict: do not add it as a general application. Add it only if one specific condition holds — that he wants a higher-probability BME-with-medical-devices target and Case Western alone feels too thin as a Rank-1 landing spot. In that narrow case Wisconsin is the right instrument, because BME's bioinstrumentation specialization plus the every-semester design sequence genuinely fit him, at ~41% rather than Case's 36% or Michigan's ~17–20% OOS.
    • If he does apply: list BME (not ECE) as first choice and use non-binding EA (Nov 1) for a January answer. BME is the better door here — unlike at UW Seattle, where ECE's Neurotechnology Pathway beat a device-less BioE, Wisconsin's BME contains the bioinstrumentation/neuroengineering content, so it needs no workaround.
    • Practical note: the honest framing is that this is a cost-and-size decision, not an academics decision. The academics clear his bar; the environment does not.

    Open questions

    • Nonresident admit rate — unpublished. Worth pulling from the Common Data Set to confirm ~41% isn't masking a materially tighter nonresident number, especially now that eligible EA residents are guaranteed admission.
    • SAT middle 50% — no class profile page found; his 1550 is presumably well above median, but unverified. Also worth deciding whether to submit at all, given test-optional status.
    • Merit aid for nonresidents — unverified, and it's the crux of the decision. At ~$62–66k with no need-based expectation, is there meaningful merit money? This alone determines whether Wisconsin is viable versus academic.
    • Per-major Core/Overall GPA minimums for BME progression — the numbers live on a CoE intranet PDF, not the public page. Worth asking, since Regulation 7's capacity cap makes BME progression non-automatic.
    • Application platform — Common App vs UW's own application not confirmed on the pages checked.
    • Culture — a large Big Ten school with a strong sports/social reputation. He has explicitly said he is "not interested in a party school or a sports school"; Madison's reputation on that axis deserves a direct look before this goes on the list, and may be disqualifying on its own.
