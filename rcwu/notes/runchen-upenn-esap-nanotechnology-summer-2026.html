    The honest admissions read (important — don't misuse this)

    • ESAP does NOT count as demonstrated interest at Penn. Penn does not track demonstrated interest, and selective privates explicitly give no admissions preference for attending their own paid pre-college programs. Framing "I attended ESAP" to Penn as evidence of fit will not be read that way. See the demonstrated-interest analysis in what-each-college-on-runchen-s-list-values-most-fall-2027-cycle (Penn is not among the interest-tracking schools).
    • It is a pay-to-attend program, not a selective achievement. Unlike free competitive programs (RSI, MITES, Clark Scholars), ESAP carries little standalone signal on an activities list. Listed by name alone, it reads as "family paid for a summer program."
    • Net: neutral as a line item; potentially strong as essay raw material.

    Where it genuinely helps

    1. Essay material across the whole list. Specific, first-person nanotech lab work (fabrication, characterization, bio-nano applications) gives him concrete technical detail for:
    2. Penn's SEAS / Bioengineering "why this major" angle (top-10 medical-device department — see university-of-pennsylvania-college-research).
    3. Cornell #1 (why engineering), Tufts engineering-project supplement, UC PIQ #6 (academic subject that inspires you), Georgia Tech / CMU "why this major."
    4. A concrete "at ESAP I did X, which reframed how I think about Y in medical devices" beats generic "I love engineering" everywhere.
    5. Field-testing the direction. Confirms (or redirects) whether nano / bio-nano actually excites him before he commits to a "why major" narrative he'll repeat across ~15 applications.
    6. Possible recommendation — only if an instructor knew his work well; a 3-week instructor letter is usually weaker than a 2-year teacher's, so do not rely on it.

    How to use it
