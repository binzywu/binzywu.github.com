    MIT College Research

    The Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) is a small, private, STEM-focused research university in Cambridge, Massachusetts (~4,500 undergrads — well under Runchen's 10k ceiling). On runchen-college-list-draft-2026 it sits in the Hi Reach tier and he ranks it a 2 ("more of a grad-school place for me"); his parents want it. See runchen-wu-college-application-profile and runchen-college-preferences-overall for context. It's a genuine reach for anyone, and the profile-vs-prestige tension here is real.

    Links: MIT Admissions · Deadlines & requirements · Cost & affordability · Biological Engineering (Course 20)

    At a glance (2025-26 cycle; verify for his cycle)¶

    Item Detail
    Total undergrad ~4,535 (2024-25); very small
    Engineering college size No separate "college" gate — admits are to MIT as a whole (see below)
    Overall admit rate ~4.5% (Class of 2028: 1,275 admits / 28,232 apps)
    Major/eng-specific admit rate None published — MIT does not admit by major
    SAT middle 50% Composite ~1520-1570; Math 25-75 essentially 790-800. EBRW sub-range not separately confirmed
    Testing policy Required — SAT or ACT (MIT is not test-optional)
    Admission type Holistic; admit to the Institute, declare major end of first year (no direct-to-major, no impacted-major gate)
    Application platform MIT's own portal (apply.mitadmissions.org) — NOT Common App
    Plans & deadlines Early Action Nov 1 (non-binding); Regular Action Jan 4
    Essays ~4-5 short essays (~100-200 words) + 4 short-response (~40-50 words); MIT-specific prompts, not a single long personal statement
    Cost (2026-27) Sticker $92,760. Need-blind + meets 100% of need; tuition-free under $200k family income, $0 parent contribution under $100k. No merit aid (need-based only)

    Fit for Runchen — the strong side¶

    • Size and campus feel fit well: ~4,500 undergrads is far under his 10k preference — one of the smallest schools on his list, and campus-centered.
    • Culture leans collaborative, not cutthroat: MIT's grading (first-semester P/NP) and problem-set-team norms are famously cooperative; not party- or sports-dominated.
    • World-class medical-engineering fit — arguably the best on his list. Biological Engineering (Course 20), Mechanical (2), and EECS (6) all feed medical devices/drug delivery; the Langer Lab and Harvard-MIT Health Sciences & Technology (HST) are global leaders in exactly the "engineering + electricity + medicine" space he's drawn to, with minimal wet-lab/dissection framing.
    • Robotics is a real hook: 7-year VEX arc with a World Championship Division title + Builder Award is the kind of deep, built-something spike MIT prizes; the MIT-specific essays reward this over generic polish.
    • Aid is generous if admitted: need-blind, meets full need, tuition-free under $200k — cost is not a structural barrier the way OOS publics are.

    Fit for Runchen — the concerns (weigh seriously)¶

    • Cold weather — Cambridge winters are cold and long; direct hit against his stated dislike.
    • Admit rate ~4.5% — a reach for every applicant regardless of stats; nothing guarantees admission.
    • Math rigor is defensible, but the credential is weaker than BC. Bellevue HS does not offer AP Calculus BC, and he takes Advanced Calculus (MAH802) senior year — post-BC, multivariable, dual-enrolled at Bellevue College. MIT reads rigor against what the school offers, so this is the top of his available track, not a gap (bellevue-school-district-math-pathway-and-advanced-calculus). The residual concern is evidentiary, not curricular: MIT's pool is full of applicants with BC 5s plus university coursework or olympiad math, and a non-AP course title carries less signal than an exam score. Make sure the counselor's profile states BC is unavailable and that the Bellevue College credits are actually registered.
    • Stats are in range but not standout-for-MIT: 1550 superscore (760 EBRW / 790 Math) sits mid-band of the ~1520-1570 range, and 3.982 GPA is strong but the single AP Lit B is visible in a pool where near-perfect is the norm. In range ≠ differentiated here.
    • Intensity: collaborative, yes, but the pace/workload is punishing — a different flavor of pressure than "cutthroat," but pressure nonetheless.
    • Drop-candidate status: he himself rates it a 2 and frames it as a grad-school place; it's on the list largely for his parents. Worth an honest conversation about whether the separate application effort is warranted.

    Placement recommendation¶

    • Category: Reach (Hi Reach) — for him and for essentially anyone; no residency angle (private, national pool).
    • Rank: 2 on his scale. Program prestige is top-of-list (a 4/4 on prestige for engineering + medical engineering), but his own environment-fit view lands at 2 — the cold weather and "grad-school-place" framing pull it down despite ideal size and collaborative culture. Clear prestige-vs-fit tension.
    • Practical notes: (1) Uses MIT's own portal, not Common App — a fully separate application with distinct short essays; budget the extra effort. (2) SAT/ACT is required — his 1550 must be submitted. (3) No major to declare at application; robotics/medical-engineering interest lives in the essays. (4) EA (Nov 1) is non-binding, so applying early costs nothing strategically.

    Open questions¶

    • Exact SAT sub-score middle 50% (EBRW vs Math split) — only the composite (~1520-1570) and Math (near-800) were confirmed; his 760 EBRW position within MIT's range is unverified.
    • Confirm the Class of 2029 / upcoming-cycle admit rate and app volume — figures above are Class of 2028; re-verify for his 2027 cycle.
    • 2026-27 essay prompts and word limits — prompts shift year to year; confirm current-cycle wording before drafting.
    • Whether the family strongly wants EA here — given his rank-2 / drop-candidate status, worth deciding early whether MIT stays on the final list at all.
