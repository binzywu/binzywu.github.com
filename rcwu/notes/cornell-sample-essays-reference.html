    Cornell Engineering supplemental prompts (Fall 2027 — CONFIRMED)

    Cornell has no university essay for Fall 2027. All writing is college-specific. Engineering requires six pieces totaling 800 words:

    #PromptWords
    1Engineering is "the application of math, science, and technology to solve complex problems" — why do you want to study it?200
    2"Why do you think you would love to study at Cornell Engineering?"200
    3"What brings you joy?"100
    4What unique voice will you bring to Cornell Engineering beyond what your application shows?100
    5Name one especially meaningful activity and its significance100
    6The award or achievement that has meant the most to you, and why100

    Prompt 1: "Why engineering?" (200 words)

    Strategic guidance (CollegeVine)

    Source: https://blog.collegevine.com/how-to-write-the-cornell-university-essays/ (accessed 2026-07-28)

    What works:

    • Focus on a specific sub-field rather than "engineering" broadly — hydraulic, mechanical, biomedical, electrical
    • Use anecdotes instead of restating the activities list — a moment that sparked curiosity or shifted your direction
    • Look toward future goals — what you want to solve/build, not just what you've done

    Good example (paraphrased from guidance, not a full admitted essay):

    A student describes watching floodwaters as a child, sparking interest in hydraulic engineering and water systems.

    Another student recounts a 3D-printing moment where curiosity turned to frustration over slow prototyping speed — shifting their goal from designing devices to designing the machines that produce them. The essay ended: the student "had woken up a computer engineer, and was going to sleep a mechanical engineer."

    Why effective: Specific, personal, and connects a past experience to a clear future direction with authentic passion.

    Bad example (from guidance):

    A student citing environmental engineering but staying "way too broad" — failing to pick one focus area or add personal anecdotes.

    Mapping to Runchen's profile

    Perfect fit: Runchen's 7-year VEX robotics journey (2020–2027) as designer-builder-programmer, culminating in VEX Worlds Division Champion + Builder Award (2024-25), gives him a concrete origin story. The pivot toward medical devices/biomedical engineering ("needs to involve electricity") — from competition robots to prosthetics, drug delivery systems, bioinstrumentation — is the exact narrative arc this prompt asks for.

    Structural model: Open with a robotics competition moment (a design failure that taught iteration, or the Worlds win that crystallized ambition), explain the shift from "winning competitions" to "solving real human problems," name the specific biomedical/electrical engineering focus (neural interfaces, powered prosthetics, medical robotics), and tie it to Cornell BME/ECE resources.

    Prompt 2: "Why Cornell Engineering?" (200 words)

    Strategic guidance (CollegeVine)

    Source: https://blog.collegevine.com/how-to-write-the-cornell-university-essays/

    What works:

    • Name unique, specific resources — labs, professors, programs, courses
    • Explain WHY each matters — connect to your goals, not just list them
    • Stay within the engineering "bubble" — don't cite dining halls or Ithaca scenery

    Good example (paraphrased from guidance):

    A robotics-arm builder connects Cornell's Biorobotics and Locomotion Lab and Professor Andy Ruina's locomotion work to their goal of designing prosthetics. The essay cites specific research areas and explains how they align with the student's prosthetics interest.

    Bad example (from guidance):

    Praising Cornell's "reputation" generically with no personal goals — an essay that "could apply to almost any engineering school."

    Mapping to Runchen's profile

    Perfect fit: Cornell's Meinig School of Biomedical Engineering is a direct match for his medical-devices pivot. Strong ECE and MAE departments support his "needs electricity" framing. Robotics project teams (autonomous systems, medical robotics) connect his competitive VEX background to research.

    Structural model: Open with a VEX-to-medical-devices throughline, then cite 2–3 highly specific Cornell resources:

    • Meinig BME — name a professor (e.g., research in bioinstrumentation, neural interfaces, medical devices) and explain how their work connects to prosthetics/drug delivery
    • Sibley School of Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering (MAE) or ECE — cite robotics/mechatronics courses or labs
    • Project teams — Cornell Baja, Mars Rover, or medical robotics teams where his builder-programmer skillset translates directly

    Avoid: "Cornell is prestigious," "Ithaca is beautiful," "I visited and loved it."

    Prompt 3: "What brings you joy?" (100 words)

    Strategic guidance (CollegeVine)

    Source: https://blog.collegevine.com/how-to-write-the-cornell-university-essays/

    What works:

    • Pick ONE specific thing — not a list
    • Be honest, not stereotypically impressive — admissions officers want personality, not resume padding
    • Evoke imagery — make them see/feel the moment

    Good example (paraphrased from guidance):

    "Sunday mornings making tamales with my grandmother" — connecting to Mexican heritage. CollegeVine notes this is "specific, evokes strong imagery."

    Bad example (from guidance):

    "Spending time with my friends" — too vague, no emotional depth.
