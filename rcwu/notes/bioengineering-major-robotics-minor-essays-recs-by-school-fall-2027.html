    Bioengineering major + robotics minor, essays & rec letters — by school (Fall 2027)

    Updated 2026-08-21
    college-applicationrunchenadmissionsbioengineeringroboticsessaysrecommendationsschool-listfall-2027-cycle

    Research across all 22 schools on Runchen's draft list (plus Rice and Northeastern, added 2026-08-21) answering four questions for the Fall 2027 (2026–27) cycle: (1) bioengineering program + is admission direct-to-major or declared later + can a BME major add a robotics minor; (2) BME-major / robotics-minor acceptance rates; (3) latest essay requirements; (4) recommendation-letter requirements. Fetched live 2026-08-17, cross-checked against the existing per-school research notes and essay master list.

    Environment caveat: research ran without a general web-search tool — findings came from direct page fetches of official sites (many .edu admissions pages bot-block automated requests). Items that couldn't be confirmed live are tagged unverified rather than guessed. Verify unverified rows in a browser before drafting.

    The three headline findings

    1. *A robotics minor a BME major can actually declare exists at only 5 schools: Cornell (MAE-run robotics minor, open to BME), Carnegie Mellon (Robotics Institute minor, open to non-SCS/CoE majors), Georgia Tech (robotics minor that explicitly lists BME as an eligible home dept), WPI (Robotics Engineering minor that names BME as a complementary major), and Northeastern (added 2026-08-21 — ECE-run robotics minor "open to all students," so BME-eligible; verified live)*. Everywhere else robotics is a major only (Michigan's BSE Robotics — no minor), a concentration/option (BU robotics concentration; RIT robotics options inside ME/EE; UCSD embedded concentrations), an adjacent minor (Berkeley's EECS "Electronic Intelligent Systems"; Penn's MEAM minor, which accepts BioE cognate courses), or nothing formal (UCLA, UC Davis, UC San Diego, Cal Poly, Santa Clara, Tufts, USC, UIUC, UW, Harvey Mudd, Case Western — robotics via electives/labs/clubs). This breaks the "BME + robotics minor" framing at the majority of his list — worth resolving before essays are built around it.
    1. No school on the list publishes a bioengineering/BME-specific admit rate. Only overall (or, at Georgia Tech, resident/non-resident) rates exist. Where BME is a capped/impacted direct-to-major program (all UCs, Cal Poly, Georgia Tech, UIUC), the real BME bar sits well above the headline campus rate — the campus number overstates his odds.
    1. Two essay changes this cycle matter: Georgia Tech eliminated its supplemental essay (Common App essay only now — was a 300-word "why major/why GT"), and Cornell made its supplement heavier — 6 required prompts, having dropped the old pick-1-of-4 optional slot and made both short answers mandatory. Also a correction: UIUC applies through the Common App, not myIllini (myIllini is only post-submission status/grades) — which removes the "separate application platform" reason that was driving the UIUC drop.

    Per-question detail

    Q1 — Bioengineering program & admission model

    No BME major named "bioengineering" at three schools: MIT (Course 20 Biological Engineering is the closest), Carnegie Mellon (BME only as an additional major or minor bolted onto a primary engineering major — can't be a standalone first major), and Harvey Mudd (one undifferentiated General Engineering degree). At all three, "study bioengineering" means something structurally different from a dedicated BME BS.

    Direct-to-major (must commit to BME at application; switching in is competitive/gated): all four UCs (Berkeley, UCLA, UCSD, UC Davis), Cal Poly (impacted, MCA formula, hardest of all), Georgia Tech, UIUC. Penn and BU admit to the school (SEAS / College of Engineering) rather than locking the specific major, but you still enter through engineering. For a student who wants to explore then commit, these are the higher-risk fits.

    Admit-broadly, declare later (better for exploring): Cornell (sophomore year), MIT (spring of year 1), Harvey Mudd (after arrival, no dept gate), Tufts (by Feb 15 of year 1), CMU (undecided-engineering option, though switching colleges is hard), Michigan (after year 1), UW–Seattle (Direct-to-College → Engineering Undeclared → place after year 1), Case Western (flexible, declare after enrolling), WPI. Case Western's flexibility and Cornell's sophomore-year affiliation are the friendliest for a student unsure between BME and a robotics/EE path.

    Q2 — Robotics minor + BME pairing

    Confirmed declarable robotics minor a BME major can add:

    • Cornell — MAE-administered robotics minor, 6 courses; open to BME/Biological Eng majors (only ECE/ME/CS majors are excluded). No admission gate.
    • Carnegie Mellon — Robotics Institute minor, "accessible to undergraduates from any major outside SCS," so CoE (where BME lives) is eligible. (Separate standalone BS Robotics launched Dec 2023.)
    • Georgia Tech — 15-credit robotics minor; eligibility list explicitly includes Biomedical Engineering. Open-enrollment via a department form, no competitive gate.
    • WPI — Robotics Engineering minor; WPI's own minor page names BME among the majors that benefit. Requires advisor approval form.

    Robotics-adjacent minors (not named "robotics"): Berkeley EECS "Electronic Intelligent Systems" minor (best formal fit there); Penn MEAM minor (lists robotics electives, accepts Bioengineering cognate credit) or CIS minor.

    Concentration / option, not a minor: BU (Robotics & Autonomous Systems concentration, transcript-noted, elective-based); RIT (robotics options inside EE and Mechanical Eng Tech, plus a standalone Robotics & Mfg Eng Tech BS); UC San Diego (Contextual Robotics Institute → embedded concentrations within CSE/ECE/MAE).

    Major only, no minor: Michigan — the BSE Robotics is a full major; there is no minor, so a BME student would need robotics as a second major or via electives.

    No formal robotics credential at all: UCLA, UC Davis, Cal Poly, Santa Clara, USC (unconfirmed), UIUC, UW (ECE-confirmed, ME unverified), Harvey Mudd, Case Western, Tufts (grad-only HRI). Robotics there = electives, research labs, and clubs.

    Where robotics minors exist, they are open-enrollment — added after you're already admitted/enrolled, no separate admission decision.

    Q3 — Latest essay requirements (2026–27)

    Changes flagged this cycle:

    • Georgia Tech — dropped the supplement. No more 300-word "why this major / why GT." Common App personal essay only. (CDS also rates the essay merely "Considered.")
    • Cornell — heavier. Duffield Engineering now requires 6 pieces (2×200-word + 4×100-word); the old "pick 1 of 4" optional short answer was removed and both short answers made mandatory. Cornell states there is no university-wide essay for Fall 2027 — all writing is college-specific.
    • Harvey Mudd — new prompts: 500 words ("an experience/community that shaped the problems you want to solve") + 250 words ("a time asking for help made a difference").
    • UIUC — platform correction: applications go through the Common App, not myIllini. Major-based short essays (~150 words each: first-choice-major experience + goals, and second choice if applicable) remain.
    • Michigan — confirmed current: community essay (100–300 w) + why-school/major essay (100–550 w, encourages naming specific faculty/courses).

    Confirmed and stable: CMU (3 short answers, 300 w each); the four UCs (4-of-8 PIQs, 350 w — one set covers all four campuses); Cal Poly (no essay); MIT (own portal, 4 essays + 4 short responses); Tufts (demonstrated-interest short + engineering-project short); Penn (2 required + SEAS-specific, 50–200 w each).

    Still living inside the Common App portal (verify by opening an application): USC short answers, Case Western general-applicant prompt, BU's two essays + Kilachand honors essay, Santa Clara supplement (verify required vs optional), RPI (BME needs no extra essay), WPI (likely no supplement), RIT (none unless Honors/Individualized).

    Full prompt wording and word limits are in the essay master list — but note that list was compiled 2026-07-25 (pre-Aug-1) and the GT/Cornell/Harvey-Mudd/UIUC items above supersede it.

    Q4 — Recommendation-letter requirements

    Subject-specific requirements (plan teacher choices around these):

    • Cornell — 2 teacher + 1 counselor; at least one teacher must be math/science/CS.
    • MIT — 2 teacher evaluations: one math/science, one humanities/social-science/language + counselor Secondary School Report.
    • Harvey Mudd — 2 teacher: one STEM, one humanities/social-science/arts; counselor optional.
    • RPI — just 1, and prefers it from a math or science teacher.

    Standard counts:

    • Case Western — 1 counselor + 2 teacher (3 total, the most).
    • Penn — 1 counselor + 1 teacher (core subject) + 1 optional supplemental.
    • CMU — 1 teacher + 1 counselor.
    • BU — 1 counselor + only 1 teacher.
    • WPI — 1 counselor + 1 teacher.
    • USC — 1 letter (counselor or teacher); some majors want more.
    • Santa Clara — 1 (teacher or counselor).

    Georgia Tech — recommendations are optional and "not considered" in the decision (up to 1 counselor + 2 teacher accepted).

    Do not accept / do not use letters: the four UCs (Berkeley, UCLA, UCSD, UC Davis — "do not require or accept," rare major-specific exceptions), Cal Poly ("not considered"), and UW (verify).

    Unverified this pass: Tufts (Common App default is likely 1 counselor + 2 teacher), RIT, Michigan, UIUC.

    What this means for Runchen — quick reads

    • If "BME + robotics minor" is the real goal, the cleanest fits are Cornell, Georgia Tech, CMU, and WPI — the only four where a BME-track student can declare an actual robotics minor. Michigan gives robotics as a major (arguably better than a minor for a 7-year VEX builder, but not combinable-as-minor with BME). Penn/Berkeley offer robotics-adjacent minors. At the rest, robotics would be electives/clubs, not a credential — so the "minor" part of his framing doesn't hold and shouldn't drive those essays.
    • The direct-to-major schools raise the BME bar invisibly. All four UCs, Cal Poly, Georgia Tech, and UIUC admit into BME, and the campus admit rate overstates his odds — especially as a non-resident at the UCs. The "declare later" schools (Cornell, MIT, Case Western, Michigan, CMU, Tufts, WPI, UW) let him keep BME-vs-robotics/EE open.
    • Two low-effort wins this cycle: Georgia Tech now has no supplement, and UIUC is Common App after all — the platform objection that was driving the UIUC drop is gone (fit reasons remain).
    • Set up teacher recs for the strict cases first: Cornell (math/sci/CS teacher), MIT and Harvey Mudd (one STEM + one humanities). One STEM and one humanities teacher rec covers MIT, Harvey Mudd, and most others simultaneously.
