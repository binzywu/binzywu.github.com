    Harvey Mudd College — College Research

    Harvey Mudd College — Greater Los Angeles, CA. A small private STEM-focused liberal arts college. Part of the runchen-college-counseling-working-school-list-student-profile; see also runchen-wu-college-application-profile and runchen-college-preferences-overall.

    Links: Engineering Educational Philosophy & Goals (hmc.edu/engineering/philosophy-and-goals); undergrad-only but with many research labs (hmc.edu/research/research-areas).

    Likes (parents)¶

    • Has ED2, and ED acceptance rate looks much higher than RD.
    • Good engineering school.
    • California weather.
    • Small private arts-and-science school — seems to care more about students.

    Concerns (parents)¶

    • Very small school.
    • General engineering degree (not specialized).
    • ED only, no EA.

    Runchen's Own Take¶

    Likes: the broad engineering program to begin with. Concerns / what matters to him: worried the schoolwork might be really hard — but he wants to be surrounded by hardworking, motivated people; good professors and classmates make everything better. He values motivated peers regardless of their major over a specific major mix. A negative experience with a condescending, ineffective sophomore English teacher (only called on the front table; unrealistic expectations, too much work in too little time) shaped what he does NOT want. Echoes his overall theme — see runchen-college-preferences-overall.

    Current-Cycle Research (2026-07-24)¶

    At ~900 undergrad, arguably the single best environment-fit on Runchen's list — tiny, campus-centered, warm SoCal, collaborative STEM culture. He ranks it his personal #1 but worries the workload is "too hard." Sits in his Reach tier of runchen-college-list-draft-2026.

    At a glance (2025-26 cycle; verify for his cycle)¶

    Item Detail
    Total undergrad ~913 (2025); no grad students
    Overall admit rate ~13% (class of 2026: 593 admitted / 4,440 apps ≈ 13.4%) — verify current CDS
    SAT middle 50% (submitted) Math 760-790; EBRW 740-770 — his 790M/760EBRW is right in range
    Testing policy Test-optional through 2026; confirm status for his (2026-27) cycle
    Admission type Holistic; NOT direct-to-major — enter undeclared, declare major end of sophomore year
    Platform Common App
    Plans & deadlines ED I Nov 15; ED II Jan 5; RD Jan 5. No EA.
    Cost of attendance (2026-27) ~$104,512 on-campus all-in (tuition ~$76,879)
    Aid Need-based aid + merit scholarships including a VEX Scholarship and FIRST Robotics Scholarship, President's Scholars, Harvey S. Mudd Merit Award

    Fit for Runchen — the strong side¶

    • Best environment fit on the whole list: tiny, campus-centered, collaborative STEM culture with motivated peers — directly answers his "will I make friends" worry about large anonymous schools.
    • Robotics spike is unusually well-matched: HMC literally offers a VEX Scholarship and a FIRST Robotics Scholarship, and the hands-on Engineering Clinic Program (real client projects for juniors/seniors) rewards exactly his build/design/program strength.
    • Stats are in range: 1550 superscore (790M/760EBRW) sits inside the submitted middle 50%; 3.982 GPA is competitive.

    Fit for Runchen — the concerns (weigh seriously)¶

    • Rigor / "too hard" is still a real risk, though less about calculus than first assumed: HMC's Common Core is one of the most demanding STEM curricula in the country. He does arrive with multivariable exposure — Advanced Calculus (MAH802) senior year, since Bellevue HS offers no AP Calculus BC (bellevue-school-district-math-pathway-and-advanced-calculus) — so he is not behind peers who arrive with BC. The workload-intensity concern stands on its own.
    • No dedicated biomedical-engineering major: engineering is deliberately general/undivided (one broad, ABET-accredited "Engineering" degree) — a weaker BME fit than a school with an explicit program.
    • No EA and no rolling — the only early option is binding ED (I or II); given the ~$104k sticker, run the net-price calculator first.
    • Reach by selectivity: ~13% admit rate makes it a Reach for anyone regardless of stats.

    Placement recommendation¶

    • Category: Reach (selectivity-driven; his academics are in-range but the admit rate and binding-ED-only structure keep it a Reach).
    • Rank: classic prestige-vs-fit tension. By his own environment-fit lens it's his #1 (size, warmth, collaborative peers). By a program-prestige/BME-specialization lens it ranks lower than schools with named biomedical engineering. Recommend keeping it a top-choice Reach and, if the family is willing to commit and the net price works, strongly consider ED II (Jan 5) where his fit-story is most compelling.
    • Practical notes: Common App; applies to the College, not a major (declare Engineering sophomore year) — so math placement is a curriculum question, not an admissions filter.
