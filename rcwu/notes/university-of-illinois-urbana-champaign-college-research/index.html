    University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign College Research

    Status update 2026-07-25 — likely drop. Runchen leans toward cutting UIUC because it requires the myIllini application, a separate system from the Common App used by nearly everything else on his list. See "Drop decision" below.

    University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC) — large public flagship with a top-5 engineering college (Grainger). Added to the draft list at Runchen's request. See also the profile and preferences.

    Links: Admissions (admissions.illinois.edu); Grainger Engineering (grainger.illinois.edu); Bioengineering (bioengineering.illinois.edu); Center for Autonomy (autonomy.illinois.edu).

    At a glance (Fall 2026 cycle; verify for his cycle)¶

    Field Value
    Total undergrad ~37,000 (very large; sources range ~34k–38.5k)
    Grainger Engineering undergrad ~5,900
    Overall admit rate ~37%
    Grainger Engineering admit rate ~21% (the relevant bar for him)
    CS / CS+X admit rate ~7% / ~17% (among the most selective in the US)
    SAT middle 50% (Engineering) 1480–1550 (82% submitted)
    Testing policy Functionally optional ("if you provide scores, they'll be used") — not formally branded test-optional; scores can help merit aid
    Admission By major — apply directly to the engineering program
    App platform myIllini (UIUC's own portal — not Common App)
    App plans EA (non-binding) 11/01; RD 01/05. No ED.
    Essays Major-specific short answers (~150 words each, 2–3) — no separate personal essay via myIllini
    OOS cost of attendance ~$58k–66k/yr; nonresident aid scarcity likely but unconfirmed

    Fit for Runchen — the strong side¶

    • Elite engineering across the board. Grainger is #5 undergrad engineering, with ECE #5, Computer Engineering #5, Mechanical #4, CS #7 — deep strength in exactly the electrical/mechanical/CS areas his robotics work draws on.
    • His stats are in range. SAT 1550 sits at the top of Grainger's 1480–1550 band; his rigor and VEX Worlds spike make him a credible Engineering (~21%) applicant — though CS/CS+X (~7–17%) would be a true reach if he targeted those.
    • Autonomy/robotics research exists via the cross-departmental Center for Autonomy (grad M.Eng. in Autonomy & Robotics). Strong research environment, though see caveat below.

    Fit for Runchen — the concerns (weigh seriously)¶

    • No undergraduate robotics degree. Unlike Michigan (which has a standalone BSE Robotics major), UIUC's robotics is research centers + clubs layered on ECE/ME/CS — not a dedicated undergrad program. He'd major in ECE/ME/CS and pursue robotics through those.
    • Bioengineering, not Biomedical Engineering, and only ~#14. UIUC's dept is Bioengineering (B.S. Bioengineering, CS+Bioengineering, Neural Engineering). It's good but ranked lower than its core-engineering programs, and no branded "medical devices / drug delivery" track was found — relevant content comes via electives/research. For his stated medical-engineering interest, Case Western and Michigan BME are stronger, more targeted fits.
    • Size contradicts his stated fit. ~37k undergrad, 20:1 student-faculty; one review flags large impersonal intro classes. Same "won't feel lost" tension as UCSD, UW, and Michigan.
    • Cold winters (Jan lows ~18°F, ~21 in snow) — colder than the PNW; cuts against his weather concern.
    • Culture (collaborative vs. cutthroat) is unverified — could not confirm from reliable sources this pass. Given his anonymity/competitiveness worry, worth a targeted follow-up (admitted-student panels, department pages, or a visit) before drawing conclusions.

    Placement recommendation¶

    • Category: Reach → Target. Engineering at ~21% is a high-target/low-reach for a strong OOS applicant like him — more attainable than the Hi-Reach cluster, tougher than his Likely tier. (CS/CS+X would be a hard Reach; his robotics fit points to ECE/ME anyway.)
    • Rank (his 1–4 scale): program-wise a strong 1–2 on engineering prestige; but on his own environment criteria (huge, cold, culture-unknown) likely a 2–3 — the same profile-vs-prestige tension seen with Georgia Tech, UCSD, and Michigan.
    • Practical note: uses myIllini, not Common App — a separate application to budget time for, with its own major-specific short-answer prompts.

    Drop decision (2026-07-25)¶

    Runchen's stated reason: the separate application system. UIUC is the only school on his list requiring a wholly separate platform. Everything else consolidates: Common App covers the private schools plus Michigan and Georgia Tech; the UC application covers all four UC campuses in one submission; UW uses Coalition. myIllini means a third portal, a separate account, its own major-specific short-answer prompts, and separate deadline tracking — for one school.

    This holds up as a reason to cut, and the platform is the smaller half of the case. UIUC was already the weakest fit among his big-public options on his own criteria:

    • Bioengineering is only ~#14 with no medical-device track — the one axis he cares most about is where UIUC is weakest. Case Western and Michigan both beat it here.
    • No undergraduate robotics degree — Michigan has a standalone BSE Robotics major; UIUC offers research centers and clubs layered on ECE/ME/CS.
    • ~37,000 undergrad and cold winters — the same objections that ruled out Wisconsin, in a category already covered by Michigan and four UCs.
    • Culture unverified — the least-confirmed item in this note, and directly relevant to his anonymity worry.

    So the platform friction is the tiebreaker on a school that was already a marginal Rank 2–3, not the whole argument. Michigan is the better keep in the elite-big-public-OOS slot: it's Common App, has the standalone Robotics major, and has a stronger BME with an explicit medical-device track.

    What would argue for keeping it: Grainger is genuinely #5 nationally with ECE #5, his 1550 tops the engineering band, and EA at ~21% for Engineering is more attainable than most of his reaches. If he ends up wanting another strong non-reach and can absorb one extra portal, this is a real option — the drop is a convenience-and-fit call, not a quality judgment. Also worth noting the myIllini burden is genuinely modest: 2–3 short answers of ~150 words, no separate personal essay.

    Freed capacity: dropping UIUC opens an application slot. Per additional-school-candidates-for-runchen-gap-analysis-2026, Olin is the highest-value use of it — it fixes the small-school gap that UIUC (37k undergrad) worked directly against.
