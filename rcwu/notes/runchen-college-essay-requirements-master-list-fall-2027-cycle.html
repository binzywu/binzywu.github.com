    Runchen College Essay Requirements Master List (Fall 2027 Cycle)

    Updated 2026-08-21
    college-applicationessaysrunchenadmissionsschool-listdeadlineswriting-supplement

    Essay and writing requirements for every school on Runchen's list, researched 2026-07-25 for the Fall 2027 entry cycle (applications open Aug 1, 2026). See runchen-wu-college-application-profile.

    READ THIS FIRST — the timing caveat

    Most schools publish their new supplemental prompts on or just after August 1, 2026. As of today (2026-07-25) that has not happened yet for the majority of this list. Every row below is labeled with the cycle it was verified against:

    • ✅ 2026-27 confirmed — the school has already published Fall-2027 prompts. Only Cornell, MIT, Rice, and Olin (timeline) are in this state.
    • ⚠️ 2025-26 (prior cycle) — prompt wording shown is last year's. Historically stable at most schools, but must be re-verified after Aug 1, 2026.
    • ❓ Not published on the school's own site — the prompts live only inside the Common App member questions, visible after starting an application. This is normal and applies to many schools.

    Action item: re-run this entire research pass in early August 2026. Nothing here should be drafted against without that recheck, except the four confirmed rows.

    The universal baseline

    Common App personal essay — 650 words max, choose 1 of 7 prompts. Common App has published its 2026-27 prompt set (confirmed live at commonapp.org/apply/essay-prompts). This single essay covers every school on his list except the UCs, Cal Poly, and UW-Madison-via-its-own-app. Written once, reused everywhere. The 650-word limit and seven-prompt structure are confirmed via UW's own description of the 2027-entry cycle.

    Tier 1 — confirmed Fall 2027 prompts (draft these with confidence)

    Cornell — Engineering ✅ 2026-27 confirmed

    Cornell explicitly states there is "no university essay question for Fall 2027 applicants." All writing is college-specific. Engineering requires six pieces:

    #PromptWords
    1Engineering is "the application of math, science, and technology to solve complex problems" — why do you want to study it?200
    2"Why do you think you would love to study at Cornell Engineering?"200
    3"What brings you joy?"100
    4What will you add to the Cornell Engineering community beyond what your application already shows — "What unique voice will you bring?"100
    5Name one especially meaningful activity, club, team, organization, job/volunteer role, or family responsibility, and its significance100
    6The award or achievement that has meant the most to you, and why100

    Note the department renamed itself: it is now the Cornell David A. Duffield College of Engineering. Highest per-school writing load on his list at 800 words across six pieces. Prompts 5 and 6 are near-gifts for a VEX Worlds competitor.

    MIT ✅ 2026-27 confirmed

    MIT uses its own portal, not the Common App — so the 650-word personal essay does not transfer here. Instead:

    Four short essays (~100–200 words each):

    1. Field of study (drop-down selection) — "Reflect on what has led you to this interest."
    2. "In what ways have you done something different than what was expected in your educational journey?"
    3. How personal and academic experiences shaped the "problems you would want to tackle with an MIT education," and who you'd want as collaborators.
    4. "How did you manage a situation or challenge that you didn't expect? What did you learn from it?"

    Four brief responses (40–50 words each):

    • "What do you do just for fun?"
    • Someone you admire, and why
    • "What's a topic, academic or non-academic, that you could talk about for hours?"
    • Generalist or specialist — which fits you, and why

    Plus an open-ended additional-information box. Activities: only four entries allowed (MIT's own form, no resume) — a real constraint given his robotics + music + soccer + clubs profile. Nine pieces total; the highest item count on the list.

    Rice ✅ 2026-27 confirmed

    #PromptWords
    1What attracts you to the academic areas you chose, and how you plan to explore them at Rice150
    2Based on your research, which "elements of the Rice experience appeal to you"150
    3Choose ONE: (a) Residential college — the life experiences/perspectives you look forward to sharing with fellow Owls; or (b) Change agents — perspectives formed by your background, experiences, upbringing, and/or cultural identity that motivate you to join Rice's "community of change agents"500

    Plus "The Box" — a Rice tradition requiring every applicant to upload a two-dimensional image of something that appeals to them. Rice states it "is not used for evaluative purposes" and should "speak for itself and not need an explanation." No writing, but it's a required upload people forget.

    Olin ✅ 2026-27 timeline confirmed (prompts not published)

    Prompts aren't public — they're Olin-specific Common App member questions. But the full Fall-2027 timeline is confirmed, and it's unusual enough to plan around now:

    DateMilestone
    Aug 1, 2026Common App opens
    Dec 15, 2026Application + Olin questions + fee due, 11:59 PM applicant's local time
    Jan 15, 2027FAFSA deadline for need-based aid
    Late Jan 2027Candidate-status notifications
    Feb 19–20 / Feb 26–27 / Mar 5–6, 2027Candidates' Weekends (CW1/CW2/CW3)
    Late Mar 2027Decisions released

    Candidates' Weekend is required: "Participation in CW is required to continue to be considered for admission." Format — Friday arrival, academics overview, dinner with student hosts; Saturday morning student-run Design Challenge, then an individual interview and a group exercise Saturday afternoon (the evaluative parts). Travel stipend provided to every attending candidate. Parents may attend Friday afternoon only and are "not to be on campus" Saturday.

    Also required and easy to miss: two teacher letters, one of which must be from a STEM teacher; a counselor recommendation; and senior first-quarter/first-semester grades sent by the school — self-reported grades are not accepted. Olin also says "A strong calculus and physics foundation is essential to success at Olin," which is where his Advanced Calculus (MAH802) senior year is worth surfacing.

    Tufts — Engineering ⚠️ Class of 2031 cycle

    Two short answers:

    1. 75–150 words — "describe how you have learned about and engaged with Tufts during your college search process" (a demonstrated-interest question — his note already flags Tufts as tracking interest)
    2. 100–200 words, Engineering-specific — "Tell us about an engineering or science-related project that you have helped build, design, create, or iterate in the past two years. What was the project and what was your role?"

    Prompt 2 is the single best-matched question on his entire list for a 7-year VEX designer-builder-programmer. Tufts "strongly discourages the use of generative artificial intelligence."

    Tier 3 — no essay at all (the easy wins)

    The four UCs (Berkeley, UCLA, UC San Diego, UC Davis) ⚠️ undated but structurally stable

    Personal Insight Questions: answer 4 of 8, 350 words maximum each. No Common App essay, no per-campus essays — one set of four answers covers all four UC campuses. UC states "All questions are equal" in review.

    The eight: (1) leadership experience; (2) how you express your creative side; (3) "greatest talent or skill"; (4) significant educational opportunity taken advantage of, or barrier overcome; (5) most significant challenge faced; (6) an academic subject that inspires you, and how you've pursued it; (7) what you've done to improve your school or community; (8) what makes you a strong UC candidate beyond what's already in the application.

    Best effort-to-coverage ratio on the whole list: 1,400 words → four campuses. UC also warns the additional-comments field is for unusual circumstances, not a fifth essay.

    Cal Poly SLO — NO ESSAY ⚠️ Fall 2026 cycle

    Cal Poly's published first-year selection criteria do not include an essay or personal statement. Factors are: intended major, college-prep coursework taken (self-reported), 9th–11th grade weighted GPA (capped at eight semesters), activities/work/volunteering with leadership, and "cognitive and non-cognitive variables" under the faculty-mandated Multi-Criteria Admission (MCA) process. Applies via Cal State Apply, Oct 1 – Dec 1; late applications not accepted.

    Two consequences worth noting. First: this is the lowest-effort application on his list — no writing at all. Second, and more important: because MCA is formula-driven off 9th–11th grade GPA and coursework, his senior-year Advanced Calculus does not help him here, and a non-AP post-calculus course may not be credited the way an AP would be. That open question from his math-pathway research remains live specifically for Cal Poly.

    Strategic read — what this means for his workload

    Total distinct writing, if he applies to everything currently listed: roughly one 650-word Common App essay + four 350-word UC PIQs + ~20 school-specific pieces. The distribution is extremely uneven.

    The heavy hitters: Cornell (6 pieces / 800 words), MIT (9 pieces, own platform, 4-activity cap), Rice (3 + an image). These three alone are more work than the other nineteen schools combined.

    Near-free applications — worth remembering when trimming: Cal Poly (no essay), RIT (none), RPI (none found), WPI (none found), the four UCs (one shared set of four). Dropping RIT saves almost no writing; dropping Cornell or MIT saves enormous amounts.

    Three prompts are nearly pre-written by his profile:

    1. Tufts Engineering — "a project you helped build, design, create, or iterate in the past two years." This is VEX, verbatim.
    2. Cornell #5 and #6 — most meaningful activity, and most meaningful award. VEX Worlds again.
    3. UC PIQ #3 (greatest talent/skill) or #6 (academic subject that inspires you) — the robotics-to-medical-devices throughline.

    Two demonstrated-interest essays exist and should be treated as tracked signals: Tufts asks outright how he engaged with Tufts, and Georgia Tech's evaluation criteria explicitly list "genuine interest in Georgia Tech." His notes already flag Tufts, RPI, RIT, Santa Clara, and Olin as interest-tracking schools — for those, campus visits and info-session attendance are application inputs, not just research.

    Sequencing recommendation: draft the Common App 650 first (it's live now and covers ~17 schools), then the four UC PIQs (live, covers 4 more), then Cornell + MIT as their own project. Everything else waits for the Aug 1 prompt release.
