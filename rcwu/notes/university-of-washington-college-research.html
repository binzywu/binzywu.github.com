    University of Washington–Seattle is a large public flagship (~35,400 undergrad) in Seattle — Runchen's own backyard. As a Washington resident he gets in-state tuition and a real in-state admit advantage, which makes UW the natural "comfortable" anchor on his list. But he's told us he "wants to get out of state," and the school violates two of his stated preferences (very large, and it's home rather than an adventure). See the profile, the draft list, and preferences. Compare the direct-admit dynamics to Michigan and the BME fit to Case Western. Tier on his list: Target.

    Links: admit.washington.edu (admissions) · engr.washington.edu (College of Engineering, Direct to College) · cs.washington.edu (Paul G. Allen School CSE) · bioe.uw.edu (Bioengineering).

    Fit for Runchen — the strong side

    • In-state economics and admit odds are the headline advantage. Resident tuition (~$13k vs ~$43k OOS), residency preference in admission, and need-based aid eligibility (Husky Promise) make UW by far the lowest-cost, highest-probability option on his list.
    • Test-blind actually protects him. With a 3.982 GPA, 10 AP exams, and a VEX Worlds spike, his file is strong on the axes UW does read; his inability to leverage the 1550 elsewhere doesn't cost him here.
    • Elite CS and strong robotics ecosystem. The Allen School is a top-tier CS program, and UW has robotics research (ECE/CSE, the Robotics & State Estimation lab, Amazon/tech proximity) — a rich landing spot for a 7-year VEX builder-programmer.
    • Weather fits. Seattle is mild and rainy, not the brutal cold of the Cornell/Case/WPI/Michigan cluster — a genuine plus given his dislike of cold.
    • Strong medical-engineering pathway — through ECE, not BioE. The BSECE Neurotechnology Pathway, the ECE Biosystems research thrust, and the Center for Neurotechnology hit his "medical engineering that involves electricity" interest precisely; UW Medicine proximity amplifies it. See the ECE-vs-BioE section below.

    Fit for Runchen — the concerns (weigh seriously)

    • Size is the opposite of his stated preference. ~35,400 undergrad directly contradicts his "<10k, campus-centered, won't-get-lost" criterion — the same objection as UCSD and Michigan. The anonymity worry is real at this scale.
    • "Wants to get out of state." UW is literally his backyard; emotionally it reads as the safe/home option he's lukewarm about, not the fresh-start he says he wants.
    • CSE is a reach even for a strong resident. Direct-to-Major CSE is capacity-constrained and brutally selective; a straight-to-CSE admit is not a safe bet even with his profile.
    • BioE is the wrong door here — use ECE (see the ECE-vs-BioE section below). BioE's formal options are Data Science and Nano & Molecular Engineering, with no medical-device or bioelectronics track, and it sits closer to the wet-lab work he's ruled out. Less targeted than Case or Michigan BME on the device axis.
    • Culture is mixed at scale. Broadly collaborative but capacity-constrained majors (CS especially) create competitive placement pressure after enrollment.

    ECE vs. BioE vs. CS — which major to list (resolved 2026-07-25)

    Verified against engr.washington.edu, ece.uw.edu, and cs.washington.edu. Two separate questions, with different answers:

    Fit: ECE wins clearly. The BSECE has a named Neurotechnology Pathway (one of eleven, alongside Embedded Systems, Microelectronics & Nanotechnology, and Sensing & Communication). ECE's research thrusts include Biosystems, and the department houses the Center for Neurotechnology, the Orsborn Lab (neural interfaces), and the Restorative Technologies Laboratory. That is a direct match for his stated interest — medical engineering that "needs to involve electricity" (Runchen — College Preferences (Overall)) — and a better route than BioE, whose only options are Data Science and Nano & Molecular Engineering.

    *Admission odds: the gain comes from not listing CS, not from listing ECE.*

    • Listing Computer Science puts him in the Allen School Direct-to-Major pool: ~630 seats/yr across all three pathways, where "the number of interested students significantly exceeds the number of students we are able to admit." Hardest pool at UW.
    • Listing ECE, BioE, MechE, or Engineering Undeclared puts him in the general DTC pool (~39.5% overall, better as a WA resident).
    • Among DTC majors the choice is odds-neutral — all DTC admits enter as ENGRUD regardless, the selection is explicitly non-binding, and UW nowhere states that first choice affects DTC admission.

    So: yes to ECE, but understand what's doing the work. Switching CS → ECE is a large odds improvement; switching BioE → ECE is zero odds change and pure fit improvement.

    Recommendation: apply DTC with ECE first choice. He gets DTC odds, a guaranteed placement into some engineering major if he meets requirements, a year to explore, and Neurotechnology as the target. The cost is giving up a straight-to-CSE admit — a favorable trade at Allen School odds. Note ECE is itself oversubscribed at the year-1 placement gate, so first-choice placement is not assured even after admission.

    Placement recommendation

    • Category: Target overall for a WA resident with his file — but split by pathway: Direct-to-College Engineering (Engineering Undeclared) is Target/Likely, while Direct-to-Major CSE is a Reach.
    • Rank (his 1–4 scale): Program prestige a 1–2 (Allen School CS is elite; robotics + BioE solid). On his own environment-fit criteria (huge size, home not adventure) it's a 3 — classic profile-vs-prestige tension, and here the fit side is what makes it a Target rather than a top choice.
    • Practical notes: Apply via the Coalition Application (not Common App); single Nov 15 deadline, no early round. List ECE as first choice — see the ECE-vs-BioE-vs-CS section above; this takes the DTC route rather than gambling the app on Allen School Direct-to-Major. Do not submit test scores — they're ignored.
