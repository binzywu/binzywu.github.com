1 · Vision

A 10.3" color e-ink slate that captures everything you think, hear, and read.

And turns it into a personal LLM-powered wiki that lives on your device.

Memex is a paper-feel device for thinkers. Write notes by hand, record meetings and lectures, read books and papers, and let an AI quietly link it all together into your own personal wiki — a living knowledge base that grows with you.

Cloud is used as a stateless processor for transcription and reasoning; nothing about you is stored on our servers. Everything lives on the device, where it belongs.
2 · Target audience

The polymath / lifestyle buyer

Knowledge workers, researchers, writers, students, and curious generalists who already own a Kindle, take notes by hand, record meetings, and have flirted with Obsidian, Notion, or Roam. They want one thoughtful object that replaces several.

The campaign will speak the language of the knowledge-worker / second-brain audience first; the music and DJ features are the "and one more thing" delight, not the lead.

3 · Hardware

The slate

3.1 Display

  • 10.3" color e-ink (Kaleido 3 or Gallery 3 panel class).
  • Resolution target: ~1872×1404 (B&W) / ~624×468 color sub-pixel grid.
  • Front light + warm/cool color temperature.
  • Wacom EMR digitizer overlaid for stylus input. No capacitive touch on the writing surface (or a recessed off-by-default touch layer to preserve writing feel; final TBD with panel vendor — see Open Questions).

3.2 Form factor & industrial design

  • Direction: Minimalist Slate. Single material body, no front buttons, no logo on the writing face. Power slider on the side.
  • Color: matte stone-grey ("Slate"). Second color limited edition unlocked as Kickstarter stretch goal.
  • Weight target: ~430 g.
  • Materials: Recycled aluminum unibody back, matte composite frame.
  • Bezel: asymmetric — wider on one edge for thumb grip when held one-handed.
  • Mic openings: discreet, top edge, two openings for stereo/beamforming array.
  • No speakers on device. Audio out via USB-C only.

3.3 Inputs

  • Stylus: Wacom EMR, battery-free, magnetic side attachment. Included in box.
  • Microphone: dual-mic beamforming array. Tuned for meetings up to ~6 people in a typical room. Background-noise suppression as a software feature.
  • Audio out: USB-C only. Box ships with USB-C to 3.5mm dongle so users have a wired headphone path day one.
  • Speakers: none.
  • Buttons: side power slider; one programmable side button (default: start/stop recording).
  • Wireless: Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 5.3 (A2DP sink, AVRCP, HFP for headset use during recording playback review).

3.4 Compute & storage

  • SoC: mid-range ARM (e.g. Rockchip RK3588 class) — enough headroom for on-device OCR, embeddings, and a small reasoning model in the roadmap.
  • RAM: 8 GB.
  • Storage tiers:
    • Base: 256 GB UFS — covers ~10 years of typical use.
    • Pro: 512 GB — heavier audio users, full music libraries.
  • Battery: ~5000 mAh, target 2 weeks typical reading, 8–10 hrs continuous recording, 6 hrs music streaming with screen off.
  • Charging: USB-C, fast charge to 50% in 30 min.

3.5 Sensors

  • Accelerometer (auto-rotate writing canvas, double-tap-to-wake).
  • Ambient light sensor (front-light auto-tune).
  • No camera (deliberate — privacy posture).
4 · Software

MemexOS

4.1 OS strategy

Custom Linux ("MemexOS"), curated app set. No third-party app store at launch.

Trade-off accepted: no native Spotify, Apple Music, Tidal, Kindle, Audible apps. Music integrations work via standardized streaming receiver protocols (see §4.4). E-reader is native, supports common open formats.

4.2 Core apps (v1)

  1. Notebook — primary canvas. Pages, notebooks, tags, layers, templates (Cornell, dot-grid, lined, blank).
  2. Capture — voice/meeting recorder with live timestamping. Hardware button starts a session that timestamps any concurrent handwriting against the audio stream.
  3. Library — e-reader. Supports EPUB, PDF, MOBI, plus annotation export. Highlights and margin notes flow into the wiki.
  4. Wiki — the LLM-powered second brain (see §5).
  5. Music — streaming receiver UI + smart-queue / ambient-DJ controls (see §4.4).
  6. Files — local file browser, USB-C and Wi-Fi transfer.
  7. Settings — including AI mode (Subscription / BYO-keys / Off).

4.3 Note ↔ audio integration

When a recording session is active, every stroke on a Notebook page is timestamped against the audio.

After processing returns from cloud:

  • Audio transcript appears as a panel beside the page.
  • Tap a handwritten word → jumps to that timestamp in the audio + transcript.
  • Tap a transcript line → jumps to the page region you were writing at that moment.
  • Cloud also produces a "merged document": cleaned-up bullets, action items extracted, full transcript collapsed beneath as expandable detail. Saved as a wiki page.

4.4 Music — the "ambient DJ"

  • Streaming sources at launch: Spotify Connect target, AirPlay 2 receiver, Bluetooth A2DP sink. Local FLAC/MP3/audiobook files synced via USB-C or companion app.
  • Phone is the source of truth for streaming auth, library, and recommendations. Memex is the player + controller.
  • Ambient-DJ features:
    • Crossfade between tracks (manual + auto).
    • BPM-aware playlist transitions ("smart shuffle that flows").
    • Two-deck queue with pre-cue on Bluetooth headphones.
    • Slow waveform / mood-gradient visualizer tuned to e-ink refresh constraints.
    • Lyrics where available (from streaming source metadata).
  • Out of scope: scratch, beat-juggling, real-time effects chains.

4.5 What we're explicitly not shipping in v1

  • Capacitive multi-touch keyboard accessory (roadmap).
  • Apple Music native browse/search (AirPlay only at launch).
  • Third-party app store / SDK (roadmap as stretch goal).
  • Cloud sync of the wiki (deliberate — see §6).
  • Camera. Ever.
5 · The LLM-Wiki

Memex's differentiator

The product's defining feature. A personal, ever-growing, queryable knowledge base that lives on the device. Inspired by Karpathy's notion of an LLM-powered personal wiki.

5.1 What gets ingested

  • Every meeting/voice recording → cloud transcription → on-device storage.
  • Every handwritten page → cloud OCR + on-device embedding storage.
  • Every book highlight, margin note, and reading session.
  • Imported docs (Markdown, PDF) via USB-C / companion app.

5.2 How it works

  • Chunking + embedding: content is chunked and embedded by a cloud model. The vectors come back; only the device retains them.
  • On-device store: SQLite + sqlite-vss (or LanceDB) for vectors and metadata. No managed vector DB.
  • Query path: when the user asks a question, the device retrieves relevant chunks locally → sends just those chunks + the question to a cloud LLM → receives the answer → optionally saves the answer as a new wiki page on-device.
  • Surfacing connections (a daily/weekly background pass):
    • Bidirectional links between recurring themes (Obsidian-style backlinks).
    • "Related notes" cards: open a meeting → "you discussed this with Bob in March, here are 3 related decisions."
    • Synthesis pages: "this week you kept returning to X — want a synthesis page?"
  • Graph view of the wiki available in the companion app (read-only against the on-device source).
  • Export-friendly: wiki can export as Markdown + links so users can take it to Obsidian / Logseq / etc. The user owns their second brain.

6 · Privacy & data architecture

Local-first, cloud-as-stateless-processor

  • All user content lives on the device. Nothing is retained on Memex servers.
  • Cloud endpoints (transcription, embedding, LLM) are stateless: requests are scrubbed from logs after processing, no training on user data, no user account tied to processing requests beyond an opaque billing token.
  • Backup is opt-in and user-owned: when enabled, encrypted snapshots are written to the user's own destination — iCloud Drive, Google Drive, Dropbox, S3-compatible, or a self-hosted endpoint via WebDAV. Encryption keys derived on-device from a passphrase Memex never sees.
  • Companion app: read-mostly + capture. Connects to the device over local Wi-Fi (LAN); no cloud middleman. Capture-from-phone (record on phone, sync to device) supported.
Trade-offs we'll be explicit about in marketing
  • No "log in on the web at app.memex.com and see your wiki." Deliberately.
  • Multi-device sync requires opt-in user-owned backup.
  • If you lose the device and never enabled backup, your wiki is gone. Onboarding pushes backup setup hard, but the choice is the user's.
7 · Cloud / AI infrastructure

Stateless utility, not a store

LayerProvider strategy
TranscriptionHybrid. Rent (Whisper API / Deepgram) at launch; self-host whisper-large-v3 on Memex GPU infra once volume justifies.
LLM (summarize, draft, Q&A)Hybrid. Frontier API (Claude / GPT-class) for heavy lifts; cheaper open-weights model (Llama / Qwen class) for the constant-rate stuff (embed, classify, link suggestion).
EmbeddingsCloud-hosted embedding model. Vectors returned to device, stored locally.
Vector storeNone. On-device only (sqlite-vss or LanceDB).
LoggingStateless; no per-user logs retained beyond 24h debug window.

10 · Open questions

To resolve before plan stage

  1. Trademark clearance for "Memex" — needs IP search. If blocked, fall back to "Cairn."
  2. Capacitive touch on writing surface — full multi-touch (more useful) vs. recessed off-by-default (preserves paper-feel writing). Final call belongs with industrial design + EE prototyping.
  3. Companion app platforms at launch — iOS-only, Android-only, or both? Both is double the engineering; iOS-only is the right Kickstarter / early-adopter bet but excludes Android-only buyers.
  4. Subscription pricing tier — final number ($10 vs $12 vs $15) should be set after a small willingness-to-pay survey on the landing page.
  5. Backup destinations at launch — minimum viable set is probably (iCloud Drive, Google Drive, WebDAV self-hosted). Dropbox + S3 can be roadmap.
  6. Audio codec strategy — record as Opus (small, great quality) by default; offer FLAC for archival users. Confirm with cloud ASR provider that Opus is supported well.
11 · Glossary

Terms

Memex
Vannevar Bush's 1945 conception of a personal memory-extension device with associative trails between everything one reads and writes. The product's namesake and philosophical north star.
Ambient DJ
The music mode — smart crossfade and BPM-aware queueing rather than scratch / effects DJing.
LLM-Wiki
Karpathy-inspired personal wiki where the LLM is both author and reader — surfacing connections, generating synthesis pages, answering questions across all your captured content.
Local-first
The architectural principle that all user data lives on the user's device(s); cloud services are stateless utilities, not stores.
