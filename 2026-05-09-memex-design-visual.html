Design Principles

Quiet by default

No logo on the writing face. No status LEDs visible at rest. The screen is the protagonist — every other element recedes.

Asymmetric on purpose

The wider left bezel is a thumb grip, not a styling choice. Hand-tested for one-handed reading and recording.

Tactile, not touchy

A side power slider and one programmable button. No menus. The hardware always feels honest about what it is.

Fewer holes

USB-C only. No 3.5mm jack, no SD card slot, no hidden reset pin. The body is one continuous surface.

Object, not gadget

Single material, single color, etched wordmark. Should look at home next to a Moleskine, not a smartphone.

The stylus has a home

Magnetic dock along the right edge. It clicks into place and never wanders. Battery-free, so it never needs charging.

On-screen UX language

The on-screen mockup above isn't decorative — it's the actual UI direction.

Reading the front render
  • Header strip — context line ("Notebook · Mar 14") + page title ("Q2 Planning Mtg"), serif typography for editorial feel.
  • Handwriting layer — the user's strokes, rendered as-is. No auto-correct, no replatting.
  • Hairline ink divider — separates user content from system layer. Refresh-friendly: a single horizontal line instead of a card boundary.
  • System line in muted gold — recording state, transcript progress, AI suggestions. Lives at the edges, never interrupts.
  • Wiki suggestions ("⌁ wiki suggests:") — the LLM-wiki proposing related notes. Subtle, tappable, easy to dismiss.

Open design questions

To resolve before tooling
  1. Capacitive touch on the writing surface — full multi-touch (more useful) vs. recessed off-by-default (preserves paper feel). Decide with EE prototyping in hand.
  2. Mic opening style — two pinholes (current render) vs. a slim laser-etched microperforation strip. The strip looks more premium but is harder to seal against dust.
  3. Wordmark treatment — etched on back (current render, subtle) vs. embossed on bottom bezel (more visible, more "branded"). Stay subtle.
  4. Stylus dock — long edge (current render) vs. top edge. Long edge keeps the stylus out of the way of the asymmetric grip; top edge looks tidier on a desk.
